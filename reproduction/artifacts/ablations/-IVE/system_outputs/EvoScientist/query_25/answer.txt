Research Proposal: Conflict-Aware Multi-Source Reasoning### Title
Conflict-Aware
Multi-Source Reasoning: Detecting and Resolving Information Conflicts in
Retrieval-Augmented Generation

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem
Current retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems treat retrieved
documents as mutually consistent and complementary. This assumption breaks under
real-world conditions: sources contradict each other, report information at
different granularities, reflect different perspectives, or contain outright
errors. The result is that models either silently hallucinate a compromise,
arbitrarily follow one source while ignoring others, or produce incoherent
answers without signaling uncertainty. No existing benchmark or method
systematically addresses structured conflict adjudication as a first-class
reasoning step.

Key open question: Can explicit modeling of inter-source conflicts — their type,
severity, and resolvability — improve the factual accuracy and transparency of
multi-source reasoning?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hypothesis
Explicitly modeling inter-source conflicts through a structured
conflict graph + adjudication module will improve both factual accuracy and
answer coherence over standard RAG pipelines on multi-source QA tasks. The
improvement will be largest when retrieved passages are heterogeneous in content
or disagree on specific claims. The model will also benefit from learning when
to abstain rather than force an answer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Method
A four-stage pipeline:

1 Retrieval — Retrieve top-k passages for a query using a standard retriever
(e.g., Contriever-MS MARCO or ColBERT-v2).
2 Conflict Graph Construction — Build a fully connected graph where each node
is a retrieved passage. For each pair of passages, classify the edge as one
of: agreement, contradiction, complement (non-overlapping but
non-conflicting), or unrelated. Use an NLI-style classifier (e.g., fine-tuned
DeBERTa on paired passages) or an LLM-as-judge (e.g., GPT-4o-mini with
structured prompting). Edge weights encode confidence.
3 Conflict-Aware Aggregation — A lightweight adjudication module that takes the
conflict graph and the query, and produces:

• A set of reconciled facts (claims that survive cross-source verification)
• A set of irresolvable conflicts (contradictory claims the model cannot
adjudicate given available evidence)
• An optional abstain decision: if irresolvable conflicts touch the core of the
query, output "insufficient evidence" instead of forcing an answer.

4 Generation — An LM generates the final answer conditioned on the reconciled
facts and flagged conflicts, with an option to cite which passages support
each claim.

ConflictFEVER is a new construction: for each claim in FEVER, retrieve a
supporting passage and a contradicting passage (by perturbing entities,
relations, or numerical values), yielding ground-truth conflict labels.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evaluation Metrics- Answer Accuracy: F1 and Exact Match on the QA subset,
answer-level accuracy on the FEVER subset- Conflict Detection Precision /
Recall: Against gold conflict labels (only for ConflictFEVER)

• Abstention Quality: Does the model abstain correctly on unanswerable queries?
F1 on the abstain decision against a held-out set of unanswerable questions-
Faithfulness: Claim-level recall — what fraction of claims in the generated
answer are directly supported by at least one retrieved passage (measured by
an NLI verifier)
• Average Inference Time: End-to-end latency, reported for practical
relevance---

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Expected Failure Modes1. Noisy conflict detection — NLI classifiers trained on
sentence pairs may fail on passage-level contradictions spanning many sentences.
An LLM-as-judge is expensive and may exhibit position bias or sycophancy
(agreeing too readily with the later source).

2 Over-abstention — The model may refuse to answer on harmless factual
disagreements (e.g., "population is1.2M" vs "population is1.22M"), hurting
usability. The abstention threshold must be carefully tuned.
3 Graph cost — Constructing a full pairwise graph is O(k²) where k is the
number of passages. With k=10 this is45 edges; with k=50 it is1,225. May be
too slow for real-time deployment without pruning heuristics.
4 Benchmark artifacts — MuSiQue and HotpotQA were not designed to test conflict
resolution; most retrieved passages may agree. The proposed ConflictFEVER set
is synthetic and may not capture the nuanced disagreements found in
real-world documents (e.g., conflicting expert testimony in medical
contexts).
5 Evaluation coupling — Abstention quality and answer accuracy trade off.
Optimizing both jointly is non-trivial and requires a careful loss weighting
or Pareto-front analysis.
6 Citation hallucination — Even with a conflict graph, the generator may cite a
passage that does not support the specific claim it is attached to. A
secondary faithfulness check (e.g., fine-grained NLI per claim-passage pair)
may be needed as a guardrail.
