Research Proposal: Self-Consistency Training via Automated Factual Hard-Negative
Generation## Title
FiND — Factual Negative Distillation: Improving LLM Factual
Consistency via Self-Generated Hard Negatives

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem
Large Language Models produce coherent but factually inconsistent outputs
— they generate statements that contradict themselves across contexts,
misattribute facts, or assert claims unsupported by their training distribution.
Existing mitigation strategies fall into two camps: (1) inference-time methods
(RAG, self-consistency, contrastive decoding) that add latency and complexity at
deployment, and (2) training-time methods (RLHF, DPO) that rely on expensive
human preference data or simple synthetic negatives that are too easy to
discriminate, leading to weak generalization.

The core gap: training-time objectives lack hard negatives that are semantically
plausible but factually incorrect, produced in a scalable, automated way.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hypothesis
An LLM fine-tuned on pairs of (factual statement, hard-negative
counterfactual) — where the negative is generated by the same model and then
perturbed via a knowledge-driven corruption process — will show significantly
higher factual consistency on held-out distributions than models trained with
standard SFT, DPO, or inference-time methods alone.

The mechanism is that hard negatives force the model to learn the decision
boundary between factually consistent and inconsistent outputs, rather than
merely memorizing factual-looking surface patterns.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Method
### Stage1: Hard-Negative Generation PipelineFor each factual triple
(subject, relation, object) extracted from a knowledge base:

1 Prompt the model to generate a natural-language sentence expressing the
triple (e.g., "The Eiffel Tower is located in Paris, France").
2 Generate hard negatives by corrupting the factual triple with four
strategies:

• Entity swap: Replace subject or object with a same-type entity ("The Eiffel
Tower is located in Berlin").
• Relation flip: Replace the relation with a semantically similar but incorrect
one ("The Eiffel Tower was built in Paris" → "The Eiffel Tower was demolished
in Paris" when relation was located_in).
• Numeric/date perturbation: Offset numerical facts by a plausible but wrong
amount ("180 feet" → "280 feet" for height).
• Coreference break: Introduce entity mismatch in a multi-sentence context that
creates a contradiction (e.g., substitute a pronoun antecedent).

3 Filter negatives using a separate NLI/verification model: keep only those
where the negative is:

• Plausible (the verification model cannot trivially reject)
• Diverse (low semantic similarity to other negatives for the same triple)

Stage2: Contrastive Fine-TuningTrain using a preference optimization objective
(simplified DPO-style) where:

• Chosen: Model's own generation of the factual sentence.
• Rejected: The hardest negative that passes the filter.

The loss encourages higher likelihood of factual trajectories and lower
likelihood of the hard-negative trajectory, using the model's own distribution
as the reference.

Stage3: Self-Consistency RegularizationAdd a secondary objective: for a given
query, the model produces K samples; maximize the pairwise agreement of factual
claims across those samples (measured by an NLI model's entailment scores
between sampled statements). This directly penalizes self-contradiction at
training time.

Dataset / Benchmark
Training data:

• Wikidata-based:100K factual triples covering entities across50 relation types
(geography, science, history, biography). Use a canonical subset with high
coverage in the model's training distribution to avoid OOD trivia.
• Auto-generated hard negatives: ~300K generated and filtered via the pipeline
above.
