 Latent-Gender Disentanglement for Multi-Type Bias Mitigation in Neural Machine
Translation## ProblemNeural machine translation (NMT) models exhibit systematic
 demographic and social biases that manifest across multiple axes: gender bias
(e.g., defaulting to masculine pronouns for doctors and feminine for nurses when
  translating from gender-neutral languages), formality bias (e.g., failing to
    preserve register distinctions present in the source), and occupational
   stereotype propagation (e.g., associating high-status roles with majority
demographics). These biases are not independent — a model that over-corrects for
 gender may introduce formality artifacts, and a model debiased on one language
                           pair may fail on another.

Existing debiasing approaches fall into three categories: (1) counterfactual
data augmentation — generating parallel corpus variants with swapped gender
markers, professions, or formality levels; (2) adversarial debiasing — training
a discriminator to predict protected attributes from latent representations
while the encoder learns to make those attributes unpredictable; and (3)
post-hoc correction — applying rule-based or learned transformations to the
output. All three approaches share a fundamental limitation: they treat each
bias type in isolation and rely on explicit attribute annotations, which are
expensive to collect and do not cover the unbounded space of
demographic-sensitive contexts. Data augmentation methods, in particular, suffer
from coverage gaps — a model trained on gender-swapped sentences still exhibits
bias in unseen occupational or relational contexts because the latent
representation remains entangled with demographic information.

HypothesisCentral claim: Demographic-sensitive features (gender markers,
formality cues, occupational stereotypes) occupy a low-dimensional subspace
within the NMT encoder's hidden representation that can be actively suppressed
via a contrastive representation alignment objective without requiring explicit
bias-type annotation or counterfactual data generation.

Rationale: Prior work in representation learning has shown that for a trained
NMT model, pairs of source sentences that are semantically identical but differ
only along a protected attribute (e.g., "My doctor is a woman" vs. "My doctor is
a man") produce encoder hidden states that diverge primarily along a small
number of latent directions. This divergence is the mechanism by which bias
propagates to the decoder. If we can construct contrastive pairs at the
representation level — pairs of sentences that share semantic content but differ
in their demographic implications — and train the encoder to minimize the
distance between their pooled hidden states while preserving the target
translation, we can collapse the demographic subspace without data augmentation.

Refined hypothesis: Applying a contrastive alignment loss on the encoder's
sentence-level representation, with negatives drawn from cross-bias-type pairs,
reduces gender bias by ≥25% (relative), formality bias by ≥20% (relative), and
occupational stereotype association by ≥30% (relative) on established
benchmarks, while maintaining BLEU within1 point of the unmodified model.

Method: CONTRA-ALIGNWe propose CONTRA-ALIGN, a training-time debiasing method
operating on the encoder hidden states of a standard Transformer NMT model.

OverviewThe method adds a contrastive alignment head to the encoder. During
training, for each source sentence in a standard parallel batch, we construct
one or more implicit contrastive pairs — pairs of sentences that are
contextually "same meaning, different demographic implication" — drawn from the
batch itself using a lightweight similarity-based mining strategy. The
contrastive loss minimizes the cosine distance of encoder pooled representations
for aligned pairs, while the standard cross-entropy loss maintains translation
quality.
