  Proposal: Decomposing Diversity — A Multi-Axis Evaluation Framework for Text
    Generation## ProblemDiversity in text generation is widely recognized as
    essential for natural, non-degenerate output, yet the community lacks a
 principled evaluation methodology. Existing metrics each capture only a narrow
   facet: Distinct-n (Li et al.,2016) measures token-level uniqueness but is
    confounded by length and ignores semantics; Self-BLEU (Zhu et al.,2018)
  quantifies intra-corpus n-gram overlap but conflates quality with diversity;
  entropy-based measures (Zhang et al.,2021) are distributional at the corpus
level and provide no instance- or facet-level signal; embedding-based diversity
(e.g., mean pairwise cosine distance) is sensitive to the choice of encoder and
    to semantic drift in long-form generation; and repetition-aware metrics
 (Holtzman et al.,2020; Fu et al.,2021) focus on degeneration modes rather than
                              positive diversity.

The consequence is a fragmented evaluation landscape: a method that improves
Distinct-4 may degrade Self-BLEU; a model with high embedding diversity may be
semantically incoherent. Researchers select the metric that flatters their
method, and reviewers have no shared standard for evaluating diversity claims.
Meta-evaluation studies (e.g., Pillutla et al.,2021; Tevet & Berant,2021) have
shown that existing diversity metrics correlate poorly with each other and with
human judgments, but no prior work proposes a unified, validated framework that
disambiguates failure modes.

Open question: Can diversity be decomposed into interpretable, independently
measurable axes that collectively explain human judgments of text diversity, and
can those axes be validated against a controlled benchmark?

HypothesisText diversity is not a monolithic property but decomposes into four
weakly correlated axes — lexical, syntactic, semantic, and structural — each of
which can be quantified by a targeted metric. A multi-axis evaluation framework
that reports all four dimensions separately will (a) reveal failure modes that
monolithic metrics mask, (b) correlate more strongly with fine-grained human
diversity judgments than any single existing metric, and (c) enable researchers
to identify which kind of diversity their method improves or degrades.

MethodWe propose Div4 (Diversity in Four Dimensions), an evaluation framework
that decomposes text diversity into four axes and provides a standardized
reporting protocol.

Axis definitions| Axis | What it measures | Operationalization |

|---|---|---| | Lexical | Rare and varied vocabulary use beyond stopwords |
Type-token ratio on content words after length normalization (slope-corrected
TTR); distinct-1/2/3 with corpus-level z-score normalization | | Syntactic |
Variation in syntactic structures | Tree-depth entropy over constituency parses
(Kitaev & Klein,2018); POS-tag bigram entropy; dependency-arc length variance |
| Semantic | Coverage of distinct meanings and topics | Mean pairwise cosine
distance over Sentence-BERT (Reimers & Gurevych,2019) embeddings, corrected for
semantic drift via centroid estimation; number of distinct nearest-neighbor
clusters in a reference embedding space | | Structural | Variation in
discourse-level organization (length, paragraph breaks, repetition patterns) |
Self-BLEU at corpus level; block-repeat frequency (contiguous n-gram
re-occurrence within a window); Gini coefficient of sentence-length distribution
|

Reporting protocolFor each evaluation set, the framework produces a Div4 profile
— a four-element vector with each axis score normalized to [0,1] against a
reference corpus of human-written text in the same domain. The protocol also
reports two aggregate diagnostics:

 • Diversity gap vector: (human_axis_score − model_axis_score) per axis,
   identifying which dimensions the model underperforms on.
 • Collapse indicator: a binary flag set to1 if any axis falls below the5th
   percentile of the human reference distribution.

Meta-validation designTo validate the framework, we construct a controlled
diversity benchmark where we systematically modulate each diversity axis
independently and measure whether Div4 detects the modulation while the other
three axes remain stable.

 1 Lexical modulation: Replace content words in a held-out set of human-written
   texts via WordNet synonym substitution with controlled frequency bins (rare →
   rare; rare → common; common → rare; common → common). The lexical axis should
   drop significantly only when rare words are replaced by common words.
 2 Syntactic modulation: Parse each sentence into a constituency tree, then
   flatten subtrees (reducing depth) or reorder constituents via a controlled
   permutation. The syntactic axis should detect depth reduction while lexical
   and semantic axes remain stable.
 3 Semantic modulation: Replace noun phrases with hypernyms (moving toward
   abstract/generic) or hyponyms (moving toward specific/fine-grained). The
   semantic axis should reflect reduced semantic coverage.
 4 Structural modulation: Duplicate random sentences within texts at controlled
   rates (10%–50% duplication). The structural axis should capture increased
   redundancy while lexical and semantic axes change minimally.

Each modulation condition is applied at three intensity levels (1×,2×,4× the
minimum detectable change) and validated by human annotators (Amazon Mechanical
Turk, N =200 per condition,3 judgments per item) to confirm the intended
perceptual effect.

Expected Failure Modes1. Axis non-orthogonality. The four axes may not be
empirically independent — semantic diversity could correlate with lexical
diversity because rare words signal rare topics. Countermeasure: report
between-axis correlation matrices explicitly; reframe as "weakly correlated"
rather than "orthogonal" if (0.3 < r <0.7).

 2 Modulation spillover. A controlled lexical modulation (WordNet substitution)
   may unintentionally alter semantic diversity (changing words changes
   meanings). Countermeasure: pre-register an "acceptable spillover" threshold
   ((\Delta \leq0.1) on non-targeted axes); flag any modulation that exceeds
   this threshold as a failed validity test.
 3 Encoder bias in semantic axis. Sentence-BERT embeddings are optimized for
   semantic similarity, not diversity. Embedding geometry may collapse diverse
   but semantically related outputs. Countermeasure: replicate semantic axis
   results with a second encoder (INSTRUCTOR; Su et al.,2023) and an alternative
   diversity-aware embedding (D4 metric from Tevet & Berant,2021).
 4 Human annotation unreliability. Annotators may not consistently distinguish
   diversity axes (e.g., rating "syntactic diversity" and "structural diversity"
   similarly). Countermeasure: compute Krippendorff's alpha per axis; if (\alpha
   <0.6) for any axis, collapse that axis with the nearest reliable axis and
   report the merged dimension.
 5 Reference corpus mismatch. Human-reference normalization requires a
   domain-matched reference corpus. If the reference and generation domains
   differ (e.g., WritingPrompts stories vs. PersonaChat dialogues),
   normalization may distort scores. Countermeasure: construct per-task
   reference corpora from the training/validation splits of each dataset, not
   from a universal corpus.
 6 Metric saturation. For very strong models (e.g., LLaMA-2 with optimal
   decoding), all four axes may approach the human upper bound, producing
   ceiling effects. Countermeasure: include the unconditional and naive-copy
   baselines as floor/ceiling calibration; report effect sizes (Cohen's (d) vs.
   human reference) rather than raw scores.

Limitations1. Framework scope. Div4 evaluates diversity of a set of generations
from the same model on the same input. It does not evaluate diversity across
inputs (topical breadth), across model checkpoints (training diversity), or
across decoding seeds (sampling stochasticity). These are distinct phenomena
requiring separate evaluation protocols.

 2 Encoder dependence. The semantic axis inherits the biases and limitations of
   Sentence-BERT and INSTRUCTOR embeddings. If future encoders shift the
   geometry of semantic representations, Div4 norms will need recalibration.
 3 Task coverage. Our benchmark spans four generation tasks but does not cover
   code generation, mathematical reasoning, or long-document summarization
   (where diversity manifests differently — e.g., algorithmic diversity in code,
   proof diversity in math). Extending to these domains is left to future work.
 4 No quality–diversity trade-off modeled. Div4 is an evaluation framework, not
   a joint optimization objective. We intentionally do not propose a composite
   score that balances diversity with quality (perplexity, factuality,
   coherence), as conflation would reintroduce the very ambiguity the framework
   aims to resolve. Users of Div4 should report quality metrics alongside
   diversity metrics.
 5 Static axes, dynamic language. The four axes are defined for English text.
   Their operationalizations depend on English-specific tools (constituency
   parsers, WordNet, Sentence-BERT trained on English data). Cross-lingual
   generalization would require re-validation of each axis's operationalization
   in the target language.
 6 Annotated judgment scale. Human judgments are collected on a Likert scale
   (1–5), which imposes linearity assumptions. Ordinal modeling (e.g.,
   cumulative link models) would be more appropriate for analysis but is not
   implemented in this proposal.

Div4 profile vector: For each model and task, the framework outputs
a4-dimensional score vector and a diversity gap vector against human text. All
scores are normalized to [0,1] against per-task human reference corpora. The
collapse indicator flags models whose diversity falls below the5th percentile of
human text on any axis.

Contribution type: Evaluation methodology + validated benchmark (±150k human
judgments across4 tasks ×4 axes).

Key differentiator from prior work: Unlike MAUVE (distributional divergence),
Div4 identifies which kind of diversity is deficient and reports axis-level
diagnostics. Unlike Distinct-n (a single scalar), Div4 separates lexical from
semantic from structural failure modes. Unlike existing meta-evaluations that
merely correlate metrics, Div4 provides a validated protocol with controlled
modulation experiments.
