EvoScientist — Ablation Experiment -IVE### TitleUnified Audio Foundation Models:
A Multi-Task Pretraining Framework for Speech, Music, and Environmental Sound
Understanding

ProblemGeneral-purpose audio understanding remains fragmented. Current
foundation models are specialized per domain — Whisper for speech, music tagging
models trained on Million Song Dataset, environmental sound classifiers on
AudioSet — with no single architecture that jointly handles speech
transcription, speaker identification, music analysis, acoustic scene
classification, and audio captioning at comparable quality. The core open
question is whether a unified model that shares representations across audio
domains can match or exceed specialist models on each individual task.

HypothesisA Transformer-based audio foundation model pretrained on a diverse
corpus spanning speech, music, and environmental sounds, using a combination of
masked autoencoding and contrastive audio-text learning, will achieve
competitive or superior performance on downstream tasks compared to
domain-specific specialist models, provided that the pretraining data
distribution and task weighting are carefully balanced to avoid catastrophic
forgetting of low-resource domains.

MethodArchitecture: A hierarchical audio Transformer operating on log-Mel
spectrograms (128 bins,16 kHz sampling). The model uses a convolutional stem
(two2D Conv layers with stride2 each) to produce a sequence of patch embeddings,
followed by24 Transformer layers (1024 hidden dim,16 heads, GELU activations). A
dual-decoder design outputs: (1) a masked reconstruction head for pretraining,
and (2) a shared projection head for downstream tasks.

Pretraining objective (two-stage):

 1 Masked spectrogram reconstruction — mask40% of time-frequency patches;
   reconstruct in frequency-domain with L1 + perceptual loss. This captures
   acoustic structure without requiring labels.
 2 Contrastive audio-text alignment — paired (audio, caption) data from multiple
   sources, using a InfoNCE loss with learned temperature. Text encoder is a
   frozen12-layer BERT; only the audio encoder learns gradients.

Downstream adaptation: For each task, a lightweight MLP head (2 layers,512→task
classes) is added. During finetuning, the backbone is either: (a) fully
finetuned, (b) LoRA-adapted (rank16), or (c) kept frozen (linear probe). All
three configurations are evaluated.

AblationsAll ablations use the same pretraining data and compute budget (same
number of total update steps).

 1 Pretraining data composition: Remove speech data (→ only music +
   environmental), remove music data, remove environmental data — measure
   per-task degradation.
 2 Objective ablations: Masked reconstruction only (no contrastive text),
   contrastive only (no reconstruction), reconstruction with L1-only loss (no
   perceptual loss).
 3 Scale ablations: Small (12 layers,512 dim), Base (24 layers,768 dim), Large
   (24 layers,1024 dim — the primary model).
 4 Downstream adaptation method: Full finetune vs. LoRA rank8/16/32 vs. linear
   probe.
 5 Positive-free impact of validation memory (-IVE variant): This experiment is
   the -IVE ablation. Compare against the full EvoScientist generation (which
   had access to accumulated validation-evolution memory) on: proposal stability
   (number of structural revisions needed), gap coverage (tasks or failure modes
   missed that the validated proposal would catch), and specificity of
   failure-mode identification. This is a meta-ablation: it measures proposal
   quality rather than model quality.

Expected Failure Modes- Domain dominance: Speech data volume overwhelms the
training signal; environmental sound and music tasks plateau. If LibriSpeech and
VoxCeleb2 together account for >80% of pretraining tokens, the model may
compress environmental sound representations into a small fraction of the latent
space. Mitigation: stratified sampling per domain during training.

 • Task interference: The contrastive audio-text objective conflicts with the
   reconstruction objective, producing embeddings that are neither good for
   generation nor good for discrimination. Expected to manifest as both WER and
   mAP degrading relative to single-objective models.
 • Catastrophic forgetting in low-resource tasks: Clotho (5.5K clips) may be
   forgotten during later training stages. Scheduled sampling or replay buffers
   may be needed.
 • Texture vs. content tradeoff: The model may learn texture-level features
   (spectral centroid, loudness) that help environmental sound tasks but that
   are poor for phoneme-level speech recognition. The convolutional stem's
   stride may compound this by discarding fine time-resolution information.
 • Without validation-evolution memory (-IVE), the proposal lacks accumulated
   knowledge of which prior architectures failed at this multi-task balancing
   problem, making it more likely to over-commit to a single objective weighting
   without sensitivity analysis. The expected signature: this proposal will
   propose fewer alternative weighting schedules and fewer countermeasures than
   the full EvoScientist proposal.

Limitations (explicit and uncontrolled)

 1 No generative audio tasks: The proposal covers understanding (classification,
   transcription, tagging, captioning) but not generative tasks (text-to-speech,
   music generation, audio inpainting). A true general-purpose model would also
   generate audio.
 2 Fixed input representation: Log-Mel spectrograms at16 kHz discard phase
   information and limit high-frequency resolution. Raw waveform models (e.g.,
   S4, Mamba) may capture different inductive biases.
 3 Limited language coverage: All datasets are English. Multilingual speech
   (Common Voice, Fleurs) and cross-lingual audio captioning are not evaluated.
 4 No streaming or real-time evaluation: Inference latency, online decoding, and
   streaming ASR protocols are not addressed.
 5 Text encoder frozen during pretraining: This prevents the audio encoder from
   influencing the text representations, which may limit cross-modal alignment
   capacity.
 6 Compute constraint:24-layer Transformer on16 kHz spectrograms is heavy (~1.5B
   parameters). Practical deployment may require distillation or quantization
   not explored here.
 7 No temporal localization: The proposal handles clip-level classification and
   transcription but not timestamp-level sound event detection or word-level
   forced alignment.
 8 Evaluation loose ends: No human evaluation for captioning quality, no
   fairness/disparity analysis across accents or recording conditions, and no
   robustness testing under synthetic corruptions (noise, reverberation,
   bandwidth limiting).
