Proposal: Functional Dissociation of Reasoning and Knowledge in Transformer LMs
via Causal Representation Interventions### TitleDisentangling Reasoning from
Factual Recall in Large Language Models: A Causal Intervention Framework

ProblemLarge language models perform diverse cognitive tasks — mathematical
reasoning, factual question answering, instruction following, and safety
compliance — using a single set of parameters. It remains unknown whether these
capabilities are implemented by dissociable functional circuits or are
irretrievably entangled in shared representations. If capabilities can be
decoupled, then targeted model editing, modular fine-tuning, and
interpretability become tractable. If they are fundamentally entangled, then
interventions on one capability will unavoidably degrade others, placing hard
limits on post-hoc model steering.

Current work (Meng et al.,2022; Vig et al.,2020; Geiger et al.,2021) edits
factual associations or inserts control vectors, but no systematic evaluation
exists that tests whether reasoning and factual recall occupy separable
representational subspaces across model scales and families. The field lacks (a)
a causal framework for defining when two capabilities are dissociated, (b) a
standardized benchmark for measuring interference between capabilities under
intervention, and (c) evidence on how dissociation scales with model size and
training data composition.

HypothesisH1: Reasoning and factual recall occupy partially dissociable
subspaces in transformer hidden states. Specifically, there exist linear
subspaces in the residual stream such that targeted read/write interventions on
the reasoning subspace affect reasoning accuracy by ≥20 percentage points while
changing factual recall accuracy by ≤5 percentage points, and vice versa for the
factual subspace.

H2: The degree of dissociation increases with model scale: larger models exhibit
more structured, separable representations, whereas smaller models (≤1B
parameters) entangle capabilities due to representation capacity constraints.

H3: Fine-tuning on a reasoning-heavy task strengthens the reasoning subspace
without proportionally enlarging the factual subspace, suggesting that
plasticity is locally concentrated rather than uniformly distributed.

MethodWe propose a three-stage causal intervention pipeline:

Stage1 — Subspace Discovery (supervised probing). For each capability
(reasoning, factual recall), collect a labeled dataset of model hidden states
from forward passes on capability-specific prompts. Train a linear probe to
classify whether the model is engaging reasoning vs. factual recall from
residual stream activations at each layer. Take the probe weight vector as
defining a capability direction ( v_c ). Validate orthogonality between
directions via cosine similarity and the dot-product overlap metric from Elhage
et al. (2022). Repeat across layers0–N to identify the layer(s) with maximal
separation.

Stage2 — Causal Intervention (subspace ablation and amplification). At the
discovered layer, perform two types of intervention during forward passes on
evaluation prompts:

 • Subspace ablation: Project the residual stream orthogonal to ( v_c ),
   removing the capability direction.
 • Subspace amplification: Add a scaled version of ( v_c ) to the residual
   stream, amplifying the capability direction.

Measure the effect on both the target capability and the non-target capability.
A capability is dissociable if ablating its subspace degrades its own benchmark
by ≥20 points while leaving the other benchmark's accuracy change within ±5
points.

Stage3 — Cross-Task Generalization. Repeat the subspace discovered from one
reasoning task (e.g., GSM8K) on a held-out reasoning task (e.g., MathQA) to test
whether the subspace is task-specific or capability-general.
Cross-generalization supports the claim that reasoning exists as a distinct
capability; failure suggests the subspace merely captures surface task features.

Expected Failure Modes- No subspace found: Probes achieve chance accuracy across
all layers, implying reasoning and factual recall are not linearly separable in
the residual stream. This would falsify H1 and require reformulation into a
nonlinear or multi-layer framework.

 • Symmetrical interference: Ablating either subspace degrades both capabilities
   equally. This would support the entanglement hypothesis and suggest
   fundamental limits on capability-level model editing.
 • Task-specific, not capability-general: The GSM8K subspace fails to transfer
   to MathQA. This would weaken H1: the subspace captures mathematical language
   rather than reasoning per se.
 • Scale invariance: Pythia160M and6.9B show identical dissociation scores. This
   would falsify H2 and suggest that representation structuring does not
   increase with capacity — possibly implicating pre-training objective rather
   than scale.
 • Safety collapse: The reasoning intervention degrades refusal rates on
   SafetyPrompts by >10 pp. This would flag an unacceptable side effect even if
   the primary dissociation criterion is met.

Limitations1. Linear subspace assumption. The probe-based method assumes
capability directions are linear in the residual stream. If dissociation exists
only in nonlinear or attention-head-specific subspaces, this method will miss
it. Results are therefore lower bounds on actual dissociation.

 2 Task coverage. Four task families cannot exhaustively represent all LLM
   capabilities. Creativity, code generation, multilingual understanding, and
   social reasoning are excluded and may exhibit different entanglement
   patterns.
 3 Prompting confound. Differences in prompt format between reasoning and
   factual tasks could produce spurious probe directions that reflect prompt
   structure rather than capability. The cross-task generalization test
   partially addresses this, but a prompt-difficulty confound (math prompts are
   harder) may remain.
 4 Static evaluation. We measure dissociation at a single inference-time
   snapshot. Training-time dynamics (how and when dissociation emerges during
   pre-training) are covered only coarsely via checkpoint ablations and are not
   the primary focus.
 5 Single intervention family. We test only linear subspace projection.
   Activation patching, representation engineering (RepE), and sparse
   autoencoder-based methods may yield different dissociation contours.
 6 Scale ceiling. The largest model tested (6.9B parameters) is an order of
   magnitude below frontier models (70B+). Dissociation at this scale may not
   extrapolate.
