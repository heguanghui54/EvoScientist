Quantization-Aware Sparse Training (QAST): Mixed-Precision Deployment of LLMs on
     Edge Devices## ProblemDeploying large language models on edge devices
   (smartphones, IoT gateways, embedded systems) faces a fundamental tension:
  aggressive quantization (4-bit weights,8-bit activations) is required to fit
within tight memory and compute budgets, but standard post-training quantization
  (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT) methods produce severe accuracy
   degradation below4-bit precision, particularly on long-tail reasoning and
  factual recall tasks. Current approaches fall into three camps — PTQ (fast,
  fragile), QAT (robust, expensive), and mixed-precision schemes that rely on
 static sensitivity heuristics — none of which jointly optimize the interaction
 between which weights to quantize, to what precision, and whether to sparsify
first. Edge deployment demands a method that navigates this three-way trade-off
                 without full retraining on each target device.

HypothesisA joint optimization over per-layer quantization bit-width and
structured sparsity — guided by a lightweight, data-free sensitivity metric
computed from the Hessian trace approximation — yields an LLM that fits within a
given edge-device memory budget while recovering ≥95% of the full-precision
perplexity on held-out text, outperforming uniform quantization, static
mixed-precision, and serial sparsify-then-quantize pipelines at the same budget.

Method: Quantization-Aware Sparse Training (QAST)

The proposed method has three phases:

Phase1 — Sensitivity profiling (data-free). For each layer, compute a cheap
sensitivity score without requiring a calibration set: the Hutchinson trace
estimator (5–10 random vectors) approximates the Hessian trace. Layers with
higher trace (higher curvature) are allocated higher bit-width (4–8 bits) and
zero sparsity. Layers with low trace are candidates for2–3 bit quantization and
structured sparsity (N:M sparsity at N=1, M=4 or M=8). This step costs
roughly1–2 forward-backward passes per layer and runs once.
