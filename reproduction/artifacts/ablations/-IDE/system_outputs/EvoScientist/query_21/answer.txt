Research Proposal: Robust AI-Generated Content Detection via
Adversarially-Calibrated Uncertainty Estimation (R-ACE)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Title
R-ACE: Reducing False Positives in AI-Generated Content Detection through
Uncertainty-Aware Calibration and Adversarial Human Text

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem
Current AI-generated text detectors (e.g., GPTZero, OpenAI Classifier,
DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT) achieve high accuracy on held-out test sets, but
suffer from asymmetric error rates: false positive rates (FPR) of5–30% on
human-written text, especially text that is factual, formulaic, or edited by
non-native speakers. This makes them unreliable for high-stakes deployment
(academic integrity, content moderation, misinformation tracking). The core
problem is that detectors are calibrated on machine vs. human distinctions from
static benchmarks but fail under distribution shift toward the "hard decision
boundary" — text that is highly structured, low-perplexity, or edited to mimic
machine output.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hypothesis
False positives arise because detectors conflate "low perplexity /
high predictability" with "machine-origin." Human-written text with low
token-level surprisal (technical writing, legal text, edited prose) occupies the
same region of the detector's latent space as AI-generated text.

Specifically: Training an AI-content detector with an explicit uncertainty head
and calibrating it on a stratified mix of naturally-occurring human text and
adversarially-perturbed human text (designed to mimic AI output patterns) will
reduce FPR@95%TPR by at least40% relative to the current state of the art,
without degrading true positive rate on genuine AI text.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Method
We propose a three-component architecture:

1 Base feature extractor: A frozen pre-trained LLM (e.g., Llama-3-8B) that
computes per-token log-likelihoods and hidden state representations — the
same backbone used by Fast-DetectGPT.
2 Uncertainty-aware detection head: Instead of a deterministic classifier,
train a deep evidential regression head (Amini et al.,2020) that outputs both
a machine-likelihood score and an epistemic uncertainty estimate. This
enables the model to abstain or down-weight predictions on inputs far from
the training distribution.
3 Adversarial human text augmentation: Construct a calibration dataset by
taking human-written text (from domains known to trigger false positives —
see Dataset) and applying minimal edits that push it toward the machine-text
region:

• Reduce token-level perplexity via greedy truncation of rare tokens - Insert
formulaic transition phrases common in LLM output - Apply "LLM-rewriting"
(GPT-4o-mini instructed to preserve meaning but "make this text look more
like it was written by an AI")

This creates a controlled set of near-boundary negative examples.

Inference procedure: Given input text, compute the machine-likelihood score with
uncertainty interval. Flag as AI-generated only if (a) the score exceeds the
decision threshold and (b) the epistemic uncertainty is below a second
threshold. Otherwise, classify as "uncertain / likely human."

Key benchmark addition: The "hard FPR set" deliberately collects domains where
current detectors fail. We will release this as a public stress-test suite.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evaluation Metrics1. Primary: False Positive Rate at95% True Positive Rate
(FPR@95%TPR) — measures false alarms at a fixed recall level2. Secondary: Area
Under ROC Curve (AUROC), Area Under Precision-Recall Curve (AUPRC)

3 Calibration: Expected Calibration Error (ECE) on the machine-likelihood
score, separately computed for human and AI subsets4. Abstention quality:
Fraction of human text flagged as "uncertain" (target: ≤5% on standard test,
≤15% on hard FPR set)
4 Robustness: Performance under domain shift (evaluate on held-out domains not
seen in training)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Expected Failure Modes1. Domain mismatch still dominates: The hard FPR set may
still be too broad. If adversarial augmentation only covers specific failure
modes, FPR may improve on the calibration domains but not generalize to unseen
low-perplexity domains (e.g., poetry, legal boilerplate from a jurisdiction not
in training).

2 Uncertainty head over-abstains: The epistemic uncertainty threshold may be
too conservative, flagging many genuine AI texts as "uncertain" and reducing
the usable detection rate. This creates a precision/coverage tradeoff that
deployment may reject.
3 Adversarial edits create detectable artifacts: The procedure for generating
adversarial human text may introduce n-gram or stylistic artifacts
(repetition patterns, specific transition phrases) that the classifier learns
as spurious features, causing it to fail on unmodified human text from the
same domains.
4 Calibration gap: Evidential regression on text may produce poorly calibrated
uncertainties due to the high-dimensional, non-i.i.d. nature of the input
distribution. The uncertainty estimates may not reflect true epistemic
uncertainty, just variations in training density.
5 Computational cost: The two-threshold inference and deep evidential head add
~2× inference overhead vs. Fast-DetectGPT, which may be prohibitive for
real-time detection at scale.
6 Adversarial human text boundaries are unclear: There is no principled way to
define "how much editing makes human text look like AI text." The boundary is
inherently continuous, and the augmentation procedure may be hard to
reproduce across labs.
