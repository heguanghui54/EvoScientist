Research Proposal: Disentangling Knowledge from Reasoning in LLMs via Controlled
Representation Intervention## Title
Disentangling Knowledge and Reasoning
Pathways in LLMs: A Representation-Intervention Framework for Capability
Decoupling

Problem
Large Language Models integrate multiple capabilities — factual
knowledge, logical reasoning, instruction following, and stylistic control —
into a single monolithic forward pass. This entanglement creates three concrete
problems:

1 Uninterpretability: When a model answers incorrectly, we cannot attribute the
failure to missing knowledge vs. faulty reasoning.
2 Brittle editing: Model editing (e.g., MEMIT, ROME) modifies factual knowledge
but can degrade downstream reasoning performance, suggesting the two share
overlapping representations.
3 Incapability of compositional control: We cannot independently upgrade a
model's reasoning ability without affecting its knowledge base, or vice
versa.

The central open question is: Do knowledge and reasoning occupy linearly
separable subspaces in the residual stream, and if so, can we intervene on one
without perturbing the other?

Hypothesis
Primary: Knowledge and reasoning activate partially disjoint subspaces
in the residual stream of LLMs, primarily in mid-to-late layers. Specifically,
factual recall (e.g., "What is the capital of France?") is concentrated in a
low-rank subspace that can be identified via contrastive probing, and reasoning
computation (e.g., multi-step deduction) occupies a complementary subspace.

Secondary: Applying activation steering vectors that suppress the knowledge
subspace (without altering the reasoning subspace) will cause the model to
"forget" targeted facts while preserving its ability to reason correctly over
new facts provided in-context. Conversely, steering the reasoning subspace will
degrade chain-of-thought quality without affecting the model's ability to answer
direct factual queries.

Method
The proposal proceeds in three stages:

Stage1: Identifying Knowledge and Reasoning Subspaces (Probing)

• Collect two families of contrastive dataset pairs:
• Knowledge probes: (question, correct answer) vs. (same question, incorrect
answer) — e.g., "Capital of France?" → "Paris" vs. "London". The correct vs.
incorrect answers differ only in factual content, not reasoning complexity.
• Reasoning probes: (question with reasoning trace, correct final answer) vs.
(same question with flawed reasoning, wrong answer) — e.g., "A bat and ball
cost $1.10..." with correct vs. incorrect arithmetic, keeping the factual
premises identical.
