Retrieval-Augmented Curriculum Distillation: Structured Knowledge Injection for
Domain-Specific LLMs### ProblemDomain-specific LLMs (legal, medical, financial)
consistently underperform general-purpose models on specialized reasoning tasks
despite fine-tuning on domain corpora. The core failure is not data quantity but
knowledge structure: standard fine-tuning treats domain documents as flat token
sequences, ignoring the hierarchical and relational nature of expert knowledge
(e.g., statutory dependencies, clinical pathways, regulatory chains).
Contemporary approaches — continual pretraining, prompt-based injection, and RAG
— each address one facet but suffer from catastrophic forgetting, shallow
retrieval, or brittle prompt engineering. No existing method jointly solves what
knowledge to inject, when in training to inject it, and how to verify it has
been absorbed.

HypothesisA structured two-stage pipeline — (1) retrieval-augmented curriculum
construction that organizes domain knowledge into dependency-ordered chunks,
followed by (2) distilled fine-tuning that interleaves chunk-level
autoregressive loss with a contrastive verification objective — yields higher
domain-specific task accuracy per unit of training compute than uniform
fine-tuning or flat RAG, while reducing catastrophic forgetting by at least40%
on held-out general benchmarks.

MethodStage A — Curriculum Construction. Given a domain corpus C, parse
documents into a knowledge graph using a lightweight entity-relation extractor
(e.g., a frozen domain-tuned NER + dependency parser). Topologically sort graph
nodes into a curriculum K = (k₁, k₂, ..., kₙ) where prerequisite knowledge
precedes dependent knowledge. For each chunk kᵢ, retrieve the top-3 semantically
similar chunks from earlier in the curriculum and prepend them as a retrieval
context window.

Stage B — Curriculum Distillation. Fine-tune a decoder-only base model (7B
params) on K with a two-term loss:

 • LLM: standard next-token prediction on each chunk.
 • Lcontrast: for each chunk, the model must assign higher likelihood to the
   correct retrieval context than to a randomly sampled distractor context from
   the same domain (margin-based triplet loss).

Training proceeds in curriculum order; each chunk is seen exactly once per epoch
(no shuffled repetition). After each curriculum epoch, evaluate on a held-out
domain QA set; halt when QA accuracy plateaus for two consecutive epochs.

Inference. Standard autoregressive generation. Optionally prepend the top-2
retrieved chunks at test time (RAG inference), but the method is designed to
degrade gracefully without retrieval.

Dataset / Benchmark- **Training corpus:**50 GB of legal case law and statutes
(public domain, English-language). This is sufficiently narrow to make knowledge
structure non-trivial yet broad enough to stress generalization.

 • Evaluation benchmarks:
 • *Domain QA:*2,000 expert-written legal multiple-choice questions covering12
   sub-domains (contract law, tort, procedure, etc.).
 • *Domain summarization:*200 legal document-summary pairs (ROUGE-L /
   BERTScore).
 • General-language retention: MMLU (full,57 subjects) and HellaSwag — measured
   before and after training.
 • Probabilistic baselines: Also report perplexity on held-out domain chunks,
   stratified by curriculum position (early vs. late).

Baselines1. Flat fine-tune — Standard autoregressive fine-tuning on the same50
GB corpus, shuffled uniformly (no curriculum, no retrieval, no contrastive
loss). Same model, same total tokens.

 2 RAG-only — Base model frozen; top-3 retrieved chunks prepended at test time
   for every example (no fine-tuning).
 3 Continual pretraining — Masked-language-model pretraining on domain corpus
   for50B tokens, then fine-tuned on domain QA. Standard retrieval-free
   approach.
 4 Curriculum-only (ablated method, no retrieval) — Same curriculum ordering and
   LLM, but no retrieval context windows and no Lcontrast.
 5 Curriculum + RAG inference (ablated method, training-time curriculum only,
   RAG at inference only) — Same as the full method but skip Lcontrast and skip
   retrieval contexts during training.

Ablations (within-method)

All performed on a10 GB subset to control cost:

 • No curriculum — random shuffle, keep retrieval + contrastive loss. Tests
   whether curriculum ordering is the critical factor.
 • No contrastive loss — keep curriculum + retrieval contexts, remove Lcontrast.
   Tests whether the contrastive objective drives the gain.
 • No retrieval contexts (Baseline4 above) — curriculum + Lcontrast, but no
   prepended contexts. Tests whether in-context retrieval is needed during
   training.
 • Flat curriculum — curriculum ordering but no topological sort (pseudo-random
   order respecting no dependencies). Tests whether dependency structure matters
   vs. any ordering.

Expected Failure Modes1. Knowledge graph noise. The entity-relation extractor
may produce spurious edges, creating misleading dependencies in the curriculum.
Mitigation: manually audit200 random edges and report precision/recall; if
precision <70%, switch to a simple section-heading hierarchy (cheaper but
coarser).

 2 Contrastive loss collapse. The triplet margin objective may drive the model
   to a degenerate solution where retrieval-context encodings collapse to the
   same embedding. Monitor embedding variance during training; if it drops below
   a threshold, increase margin or switch to a negative sampling strategy with
   hard-negatives from the same curriculum week.
 3 Curriculum early-chunk overfitting. Early chunks are smaller and seen more
   times per epoch. If perplexity on early positions is significantly lower than
   on late positions, subsample early chunks or add a curriculum-position weight
   to LLM.
 4 MMLU regression despite method gains. If domain QA improves but MMLU drops
   >5%, the domain corpus is inducing forgetting. Proposed mitigation: replay5%
   general-domain data per curriculum step (recorded as a variant analysis if
   triggered).

Limitations1. Single domain. Results may not generalize to domains with flatter
knowledge structures (e.g., conversational customer support). Cross-domain
replication is left to future work.

 2 No human evaluation. Domain QA accuracy is expert-written but
   multiple-choice. Open-ended generation quality and expert-judged correctness
   are not measured, which may overstate practical utility.
 3 Curriculum construction cost. Knowledge graph extraction and topological
   sorting add ~2× preprocessing cost over flat tokenization. The efficiency
   argument rests on reduced training tokens to convergence, not on total
   end-to-end cost.
 4 7B ceiling. Scaling behavior to70B+ models is unknown. The contrastive loss
   and curriculum design may behave differently at scale where models
   internalize structure with less explicit guidance.
 5 Single-pass curriculum. The method does not adapt the curriculum based on
   which chunks the model struggles with. An adaptive curriculum that re-orders
   based on validation loss is a natural extension but is not tested here.
