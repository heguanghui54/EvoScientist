Grounded Decoding via Dual-Channel Factual Verification for Consistent LLM
    Generation## ProblemLarge Language Models generate text that is fluent,
   coherent, and often indistinguishable from human-written prose — yet they
routinely produce statements that are factually incorrect. These hallucinations
 persist across model scales, architectures, and decoding strategies, even when
   the model "knows" the correct answer in its parameters. The problem is not
merely occasional factual errors: it is a systematic failure to reliably ground
 generation in verifiable facts. This undermines trust in any application where
  accuracy matters, from question answering to report generation to scientific
                                 communication.

Existing approaches treat factual consistency as a post-hoc filter
(retrieve-then-verify), a training-time objective (RLHF, DPO), or a
retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline. Each has a blind spot: post-hoc
filters cannot intervene during generation; training-time methods are expensive
and brittle under distribution shift; RAG retrieves relevant passages but does
not guarantee the model adheres to them during decoding. No current method
jointly constrains decoding in real time while verifying generated content
against retrieved evidence at the token and sentence level.

HypothesisA dual-channel architecture that combines (1) real-time grounded
decoding — biasing token probabilities toward span completions that align with
retrieved factual evidence — with (2) sentence-level factual consistency scoring
that modulates a dynamic trade-off between fluency and faithfulness during
generation, will reduce factual errors in LLM outputs by at least40% relative to
standard decoding approaches while maintaining competitive fluency (perplexity
within5% of the unconstrained baseline).

The key claim is that factual consistency is not just a retrieval problem or a
training problem, but a decoding-time control problem: the model already encodes
relevant facts, but standard autoregressive decoding does not enforce that the
generated surface form respects those facts. Placing a lightweight factual
consistency classifier in the decoding loop provides the missing constraint.

MethodThe proposed system, FactGround, operates at three levels of granularity:

Level1 — Contextualized Retrieval and Span Alignment. For each input prompt,
retrieve the top-k fact units from a structured knowledge base
(Wikipedia-derived relation triples + entity-linked passages). Encode each fact
unit with the LLM's hidden states via cross-attention. During decoding, maintain
a set of active fact spans — sub-sequences of the retrieved facts that overlap
with the current generation context. The decoder produces a grounded logit
distribution by interpolating the base LLM's distribution with a distribution
