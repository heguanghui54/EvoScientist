LongRoPE-Extended: Token-Level Positional Granularity for Extreme Long-Context
 LLMs## ProblemLarge Language Models (LLMs) continue to struggle with coherent
 reasoning over very long sequences (128K+ tokens). Existing position encoding
 extensions—RoPE, ALiBi, YaRN, NTK-aware scaling—either saturate in perplexity
    beyond a critical context length or exhibit a sharp "lost-in-the-middle"
     degradation where information placed at the center of long contexts is
 effectively invisible to the model. Current approaches treat position encoding
as a monolithic interpolation (frequency scaling), which fails because different
   attention heads and different token positions require distinct degrees of
    position sensitivity. The core problem is that positional expressiveness
  collapses as the sequence grows: distant positions become indistinguishable
under uniform frequency scaling, destroying the model's ability to retrieve and
                     compose information across long spans.

HypothesisDecomposing position encoding into head-adaptive frequency bands and
token-level position gating will preserve long-context perplexity and retrieval
accuracy beyond128K tokens. Specifically, each attention head selects a learned
mixture of low-frequency (coarse, long-range) and high-frequency (fine, local)
position components via a lightweight head-specific gating vector. Additionally,
each token learns to dynamically modulate its position sensitivity through a
context-dependent position-awareness gate conditioned on its own hidden state.
This dual mechanism jointly ensures that (a) heads specializing in long-range
dependency can maintain discriminative position signals even at extreme
distances, and (b) tokens in information-dense regions can sharpen their
position resolution while tokens in sparse regions can generalize across longer
gaps.

MethodLongRoPE-Extended modifies the rotary position encoding (RoPE) in an
existing long-context LLM (e.g., Llama-3-70B with YaRN scaling) with two
additive modules:

 1 Head-Adaptive Frequency Decomposition. Instead of applying a single frequency
   set to all attention heads, we maintain a bank of K base frequency groups
   (e.g., K=4), each with distinct rotary frequencies covering different ranges.
   Each head learns a soft assignment vector α_h ∈ R^K (via a lightweight MLP
   with a softmax output) that mixes these frequency groups at every forward
   pass. This allows head5, for instance, to attend strongly to nearby tokens
   (high-frequency mixture) while head20 attends to document-level dependencies
   (low-frequency mixture)—all within the same layer and without increasing the
   total parameter count beyond the small assignment MLP (≈0.01% of total
   params).
 2 Token-Level Position Gating. After RoPE is applied, each token computes a
   scalar position-awareness gate g_t = σ(W_g h_t + b_g) where h_t is the
   token's hidden state before the attention computation. The position encoding
   is then modulated: PE'_t = g_t · PE_t + (1 - g_t) · PĒ, where PĒ is a learned
   position-agnostic embedding. Intuitively, tokens that carry critical
   information (e.g., a cited number or a named entity) can "dial up" their
   position sensitivity to maintain fine-grained retrieval, while filler tokens
   (e.g., stopwords in the middle of a long passage) can relax positional
   constraints to reduce noise. This gate is applied independently per token per
   layer.

Training. We fine-tune Llama-3-70B on a mixture of three data sources: (a) the
Long-Synthetic dataset (randomly inserted key-value facts at varying depths),
(b) subsets of Proof-Pile-2 filtered for documents >64K tokens, and (c)
carefully selected slices of RedPajama-V2 with artificially inserted
cross-document dependency chains. We train for8K steps with a linear warmup and
cosine decay, using a learning rate of2e-5 and a batch size of128.

Inference. No overhead beyond the per-head soft assignment and per-token sigmoid
gate—both add <0.5% latency on a single A100-80GB at128K context.

Dataset / BenchmarkWe evaluate on the full suite of the RULER benchmark
(Needle-in-a-Haystack variants for retrieval, multi-needle, multi-query, and
variable-token-position tasks) and on LongBench (English subsets: QMSum, Qasper,
NarrativeQA, HotpotQA, MultiFieldQA-En, TREC, TriviaQA, SAMSum, and GovReport).
We additionally include the SCROLLS dataset (ContractNLI, SummScreen) and the
L-Eval suite for long-document QA. For stress-testing beyond128K, we construct a
synthetic "Mosaic Retrieval" task inspired by the work of Liu et al. (2024):
facts are inserted at20 equally spaced positions across a256K-token sequence,
and the model must retrieve all of them. This test exposes whether positional
resolution collapses at extreme lengths.

Evaluation Metrics- Primary: Passkey retrieval accuracy (single needle) at
depths0%,25%,50%,75%,100% across sequence lengths4K,8K,16K,32K,64K,128K, and256K
tokens. Reported as accuracy per depth bin.

 • Secondary: LongBench average F1 / Rouge-L score across all subsets.
 • Tertiary: Mosaic Retrieval F1 (mean retrieval accuracy over all20 needles
   per256K sequence).
 • Diagnostic: Per-head frequency diversity (entropy of α_h distributions across
   heads), position-gate statistics (mean and variance of g_t per token type,
   per position bin, and per layer). These diagnostics are used only for
   analysis, not as optimization targets.

Baselines| Method | Description |

|---|---| | Full-RoPE (no scaling) | Original Llama-3-70B without any extension
(max8K context, evaluated at all lengths regardless—fails beyond8K, establishes
the unextended floor). | | YaRN | Frequency interpolation with temperature
scaling. A direct frequency-engineering competitor. | | NTK-aware | Neural
Tangent Kernel-inspired frequency scaling. | | SelfExtend | Grouped attention
with two position scopes. | | LongRoPE | The non-evolutionary baseline that
inspired this work—learned frequency rescaling (no head-adaptive or token-level
components). |

All baselines are evaluated under identical fine-tuning conditions (same data,
same8K-step schedule, same LoRA rank-64 setup for comparability). Full
fine-tuning is used only for the proposed method; baselines use LoRA to keep
compute feasible.

Ablations1. w/o Head-Adaptive Frequencies. Remove the head-specific frequency
assignment; all heads share a single trained frequency bank. Tests whether
head-specific mixing is essential or just a result of overparameterization.

 2 w/o Token-Level Gate. Set g_t =1 for all tokens, reverting to unmodulated
   position encoding. Tests whether token-level position modulation matters
   beyond the head-adaptive frequencies.
 3 Static Frequency Bank. Replace the learned α_h with a fixed hand-designed
   assignment (half heads low-frequency, half high-frequency). Tests whether the
   soft assignment is learning anything beyond a static partition.
 4 Single Training Stage. Remove the data-mixture curriculum (present all data
   from step0) to test whether the training schedule interacts with the position
   gating mechanism.
 5 Parameter Count Control. Reduce the base model's hidden dimension by0.01%
   (matching the added gate parameters) to verify that improvements are not
   simply from adding parameters.

Expected Failure Modes- Frequency collapse. The soft assignment MLP may converge
to a uniform mixture across heads, effectively reverting to the single-frequency
baseline. We mitigate this by initializing α_h with a Dirichlet prior that
favors sparse assignments and monitoring per-head entropy during training as a
diagnostic.

 • Gate saturation. The token-level gates may saturate to either0 or1 for all
   tokens, eliminating the modulation effect. We counter this by adding a small
   L2 penalty on (g_t -0.5)^2 during the first1K training steps to encourage
   exploration, then releasing the penalty.
 • Instability at extreme lengths. Position-gating conditioned on hidden states
   may produce divergent behavior on sequences longer than any seen during
   training. We include256K-length synthetic data in the training mixture (≈5%
   of steps) to expose the model to extreme positions during fine-tuning.
 • Benchmark contamination. LongBench subsets may overlap with the RedPajama-V2
   training data. We explicitly filter against the LongBench evaluation sets
   using13-gram overlap deduplication (following the standard practice of Chen
   et al.,2024) and report any remaining overlap ratios.
