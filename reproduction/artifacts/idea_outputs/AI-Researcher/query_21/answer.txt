**Title**: Perturbation-Invariant Consistency Checking (PICC): Reducing False Positives in AI-Generated Text Detection via Semantic Stability Analysis.

**Problem and Motivation**
Current AI-generated content (AIGC) detectors suffer from high False Positive Rates (FPR), particularly when evaluating highly structured human writing, such as technical documentation, legal text, or essays written by non-native English speakers (L2 learners). This "predictability bias" occurs because detectors often equate low perplexity and low burstiness with machine generation. In high-stakes environments like academia or journalism, a false positive can lead to unjust accusations of misconduct. There is a critical need for a detection framework that differentiates between "predictable human prose" and "machine-generated sequences."

**Core Hypothesis**
AI-generated text resides on a stable, low-dimensional manifold defined by the underlying model's probability distribution. Therefore, semantic-preserving perturbations (e.g., synonym substitution, passive-to-active voice conversion) will result in minimal shifts in the detection score for true AI content. In contrast, human-written "false positives" are statistical outliers; slight perturbations will cause their detection scores to regress toward the mean (human-like), exhibiting high variance across perturbations.

**Method**
The PICC framework operates as a wrapper around any base logit-based or classifier-based detector:
1.  **Input**: A candidate text $T$.
2.  **Perturbation Generation**: Generate $N$ semantic-preserving variants $\{T_1, T_2, ..., T_n\}$ using a T5-based paraphraser or controlled heuristic rules (e.g., back-translation).
3.  **Consistency Scoring**: Pass the original text and all $N$ variants through a base detector $D$ to obtain scores $S = \{D(T), D(T_1), ..., D(T_n)\}$.
4.  **Stability Filtering**:
    *   Calculate the **Stability Index** ($\sigma$): The inverse of the variance of $S$.
    *   Calculate the **Mean Shift** ($\Delta$): The difference between $D(T)$ and the mean of $S$.
5.  **Classification**: A sample is classified as AI only if $D(T) > \text{threshold}$ AND $\sigma > \text{stability\_threshold}$. If the score drops significantly under perturbation, it is flagged as a likely human-written false positive.

**Dataset or Benchmark**
The proposal utilizes a composite benchmark designed to stress-test false positives:
*   **Human Samples**: The *L2-Writing Dataset* (essays by non-native speakers) and the *PubMed Abstracts* collection (highly structured technical text).
*   **AI Samples**: Text generated by GPT-4, Claude 3.5, and Llama-3, using diverse prompts (creative, technical, and academic).
*   **Adversarial Human Text**: The "Human-written-but-boring" subset of the *MGTBench*.

**Evaluation Metrics**
*   **FPR at 95% TPR**: The primary metric, measuring how many human samples are misidentified when the detector is tuned to catch 95% of AI samples.
*   **Partial Area Under the ROC Curve (pAUC)**: Focused specifically on the low-FPR region (0.0 to 0.1).
*   **Expected Calibration Error (ECE)**: To measure how well the detection probability reflects actual accuracy.
*   **Stability-to-Noise Ratio**: Correlation between perturbation intensity and score degradation.

**Baselines**
1.  **Likelihood-based**: DetectGPT (Mitchell et al.).
2.  **Classifier-based**: RoBERTa-large-detector.
3.  **Statistical**: Binomial-test-based perplexity measures.
4.  **Commercial Proxy**: Simulated results mimicking the behavior of zero-shot classifiers (e.g., GPTZero-style thresholds).

**Ablations**
*   **Perturbation Strategy**: Compare back-translation vs. LLM-based paraphrasing vs. random word-masking.
*   **Sample Size ($N$)**: Evaluate the trade-off between the number of perturbations (computational cost) and the reduction in FPR.
*   **Base Detector**: Swap the underlying detection model to test if PICC is model-agnostic.

**Failure Modes and Feasibility Risks**
*   **Computational Overhead**: Generating $N$ perturbations and running $N+1$ inference passes increases latency, making it less suitable for real-time API filtering without optimization.
*   **Over-Smoothing**: If the paraphraser is too aggressive, it may turn AI-generated text into human-like text, increasing False Negatives (FN).
*   **Deterministic Paraphrasers**: If the perturbation tool itself is an LLM, it might introduce its own "AI-signature" into the variants, potentially biasing the consistency score. This will be mitigated by using rule-based augmentation for a subset of the perturbations.
