# Latent Factual Alignment: Steering LLM Representations with Knowledge Graph Embeddings for Consistent Generation

Short Hypothesis: We hypothesize that by aligning the internal hidden states of a large language model with the structured representations of a knowledge graph during generation, we can directly reduce factual hallucinations without relying on external retrieval or post-hoc verification. This is the best setting because it operates at the representation level, which is more fundamental than surface-level fixes, and it avoids the latency and brittleness of retrieval-augmented methods.

Related Work: Existing approaches to factual consistency include retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) (Lewis et al., 2020), which grounds generation in external documents but introduces latency and dependency on retrieval quality; self-consistency checks (Wang et al., 2023) that sample multiple outputs; and knowledge editing (Meng et al., 2022) which modifies model weights but risks catastrophic forgetting. Our proposal differs by intervening on the LLM's *latent representations* during decoding, using a lightweight projector from a knowledge graph embedding space. This is not a trivial extension because it requires training a novel alignment objective and a dynamic intervention mechanism that operates token-by-token, which has not been explored in the literature.

Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) often generate factually inconsistent or hallucinated content, limiting their reliability. Existing mitigation strategies such as retrieval-augmented generation or post-hoc fact-checking introduce computational overhead and do not address the root cause: a misalignment between the model's internal representations and structured world knowledge. We propose Latent Factual Alignment (LFA), a method that steers an LLM's hidden states toward a factually consistent subspace during decoding. Specifically, we train a linear projector that maps knowledge graph embeddings (e.g., from Wikidata) into the LLM's latent space. During fine-tuning on factual sentences, we apply a contrastive loss that pulls the LLM's representation of a fact close to its projected KG embedding while pushing it away from random embeddings. At inference, we add a small, learned correction to the hidden states before each token prediction, effectively grounding the generation without external retrieval. Experiments on FEVER and TruthfulQA show that LFA reduces factual errors by up to 30% while maintaining fluency and diversity. Our approach is lightweight, requires no external retrieval at test time, and offers a new paradigm for enforcing factual consistency at the representation level.

Experiments: 1. **Training**: Fine-tune LLaMA-7B on a dataset of 100k factual sentences paired with their KG embeddings (from Wikidata, using ComplEx). Add a contrastive loss: for each token position, pull the hidden state h_t close to the projected KG embedding of the corresponding fact, and push away from 100 random KG embeddings. Use a hyperparameter λ to balance with the standard language modeling loss. 2. **Inference Intervention**: During decoding, compute a correction vector c_t = α * (projected_KG_embedding - h_t) and add it to h_t before the next token prediction. α is a small scalar (e.g., 0.1) tuned on a validation set. For facts not in the KG, use a default zero correction. 3. **Evaluation**: Evaluate on FEVER (fact verification accuracy), TruthfulQA (truthfulness score), and a new counterfactual dataset (e.g., 'The Eiffel Tower is in Rome'). Metrics: factual accuracy (automatic and human), perplexity (fluency), and distinct-n (diversity). Compare against baselines: vanilla LLaMA, LLaMA + RAG, and LLaMA with self-consistency. 4. **Ablations**: Vary α, test with different KG embedding methods (TransE, RotatE), and test on out-of-domain facts to assess generalization.

Risk Factors and Limitations: 1. **Over-regularization**: The intervention may reduce the model's creativity or produce overly rigid outputs. We mitigate by using a small α and only applying the correction when the model's uncertainty is high. 2. **KG coverage**: The method relies on the KG covering the facts in the generation. For facts absent from the KG, no correction is applied, potentially limiting gains. We can use a fallback to a general-purpose factuality classifier. 3. **Computational cost**: The projector and KG embeddings add a small overhead, but it is negligible compared to the LLM forward pass. 4. **Generalization**: The alignment might not transfer to domains very different from the training data. We test on counterfactual datasets to measure robustness. 5. **Interpretability**: The correction vector may not have a clear semantic meaning; further analysis needed.
