# Learning General-Purpose Audio Representations by Minimizing Conditional Entropy of Augmented Views

Short Hypothesis: We hypothesize that a powerful and general-purpose audio foundation model can be learned by simply minimizing the conditional entropy of latent representations across different audio augmentations, without relying on contrastive negatives, reconstruction, or explicit clustering. This approach, which we call Audio Conditional Entropy Minimization (ACEM), forces the model to extract only the information that is invariant to augmentations (e.g., pitch shift, time stretch, background noise, and speed) while discarding irrelevant details. This is the best setting to investigate because it directly targets the core goal of representation learning—extracting semantically meaningful, invariant features—without the complexity of negative sampling or reconstruction loss balancing. There is no obvious simpler way to achieve this: other methods like contrastive learning require careful negative sampling strategies, and masked prediction (e.g., HuBERT) requires clustering or teacher models.

Related Work: Current audio foundation models primarily rely on contrastive learning (e.g., CLAP, COLA) or masked prediction (e.g., HuBERT, WavLM). Contrastive methods require large batches and careful negative engineering. Masked prediction methods require iterative clustering or teacher-student distillation. Our proposal differs by using a purely information-theoretic objective: minimize the conditional entropy H(Z|Z') where Z and Z' are representations of two augmented views of the same audio. This is inspired by the conditional entropy minimization (CEM) principle in vision (e.g., VICReg, Barlow Twins), but we adapt it to audio with domain-specific augmentations and a novel stochastic augmentation pipeline. Unlike prior work, we do not use any explicit variance or covariance regularization; the entropy minimization naturally encourages compact, invariant representations. This is not a trivial extension because audio augmentations (e.g., pitch shift, reverberation, mixing) are fundamentally different from image augmentations and require careful design to preserve semantic content.

Abstract: We propose Audio Conditional Entropy Minimization (ACEM), a novel self-supervised learning framework for general-purpose audio representation learning. ACEM directly minimizes the conditional entropy of latent representations across stochastically augmented views of the same audio clip. This simple objective forces the encoder to discard nuisance variations (e.g., pitch, speed, background noise) while preserving semantic content (e.g., speech content, musical instrument, environmental sound class). Unlike contrastive methods, ACEM requires no negative pairs, no large batch sizes, and no memory banks. Unlike masked prediction methods, it requires no clustering or teacher models. We design a diverse augmentation pipeline tailored to audio, including pitch shift, time stretch, reverberation, mixing with noise, and equalization. We evaluate ACEM on a suite of downstream tasks: speech recognition (LibriSpeech), speaker identification (VoxCeleb), music instrument classification (NSynth), and environmental sound classification (ESC-50). We show that ACEM matches or outperforms HuBERT and CLAP on all tasks while being significantly simpler to train. Our work demonstrates that information-theoretic principles can yield state-of-the-art audio representations without complex training paradigms.

Experiments: 1. **Pre-training**: Use AudioSet (2M clips) and LibriSpeech (960h) as unlabeled data. Train a ResNet-50 or a small Transformer encoder (e.g., 12-layer, 512-dim) with ACEM objective: minimize H(Z|Z') via a variational upper bound using a simple MLP predictor that estimates the conditional distribution. Specifically, we use a Gaussian likelihood: p(Z|Z') = N(Z; f(Z'), sigma^2 I), and minimize the negative log-likelihood. This is equivalent to MSE between Z and f(Z'). We apply stop-gradient to Z' to prevent collapse (like BYOL). Augmentations: pitch shift (+/- 3 semitones), time stretch (0.8x-1.2x), reverberation (random RIR), additive noise (random clips from AudioSet), and random equalization. Each augmentation is applied with 50% probability.

2. **Evaluation**: Linear probe evaluation on frozen representations for: (a) speech recognition (LibriSpeech test-clean, word error rate), (b) speaker identification (VoxCeleb1, accuracy), (c) music instrument classification (NSynth, accuracy), (d) environmental sound classification (ESC-50, accuracy). Also evaluate fine-tuning on full datasets.

3. **Ablations**: (a) Remove each augmentation type to measure contribution. (b) Compare to contrastive (SimCLR-style) and masked prediction (HuBERT) baselines. (c) Vary the predictor architecture (linear vs MLP). (d) Test different entropy estimation methods (e.g., using a normalizing flow).

4. **Analysis**: Compute mutual information between representations and downstream labels using MINE estimator. Visualize t-SNE of representations. Measure invariance to augmentations by predicting augmentation parameters from representations.

Risk Factors and Limitations: 1. **Collapse risk**: Without careful design, the model may learn trivial constant representations. We mitigate this with stop-gradient and a predictor network, but collapse may still occur. We will monitor representation variance and add a small variance regularization if needed.
2. **Augmentation sensitivity**: Performance may heavily depend on the choice of augmentations. If augmentations are too aggressive, semantic information may be lost. We will carefully tune augmentation magnitudes.
3. **Computational cost**: Training on AudioSet requires significant compute (e.g., 4 GPUs for 1 week). However, this is standard for audio foundation models and within academic lab budgets.
4. **Limited evaluation**: We only evaluate on a subset of tasks. General-purpose claims require broader evaluation (e.g., audio captioning, sound event detection). We will add more tasks if time permits.
5. **Theoretical justification**: The conditional entropy minimization objective is a heuristic; we do not prove convergence or optimality. We rely on empirical validation.
