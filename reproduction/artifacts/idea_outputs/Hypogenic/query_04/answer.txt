**1. Title**
Adversarial Deliberation: Multi-Agent Consensus for High-Risk Clinical Decision Support in Multi-Morbid Patients.

**2. Problem and Motivation**
Medical guidelines are typically designed for single-disease management. However, a significant portion of the patient population is multi-morbid (e.g., having both chronic kidney disease and heart failure). When guidelines for these conditions conflict—such as one recommending aggressive fluid resuscitation and the other requiring fluid restriction—standard AI models often provide "averaged" or sycophantic advice that ignores critical contraindications. Current Large Language Model (LLM) agents often prioritize a coherent-sounding narrative over the rigorous identification of clinical contradictions.

**3. Core Hypothesis**
A multi-agent system utilizing "adversarial deliberation"—where individual agents are assigned distinct, specialized medical roles (e.g., Cardiologist vs. Nephrologist) and forced to debate conflicting treatments—will identify significantly more potential medical contraindications than a single-agent LLM with an equivalent context window and chain-of-thought reasoning.

**4. Method**
The proposed system, **AdvMed-Agent**, operates in three stages:
1.  **Role-Based Decomposition:** Given a patient case with conflicting comorbidities, the system instantiates two specialist agents (e.g., Agent A: Cardiology; Agent B: Nephrology) and one Synthesizer agent.
2.  **Adversarial Debate:** Each specialist agent is prompted to advocate strictly for their respective guideline. They are then presented with the other agent's proposed treatment and instructed to find "safety violations" or "contraindications" based on their specific domain knowledge.
3.  **Synthesized Resolution:** The Synthesizer agent receives the full transcript of the debate. Its objective is not to "compromise," but to generate a final recommendation that explicitly lists the risks identified during the debate and selects the path with the lowest mortality risk based on provided evidence.

**5. Dataset or Benchmark**
*   **MIMIC-IV (Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care):** Use de-identified electronic health records to extract cases of patients with at least two conflicting chronic conditions.
*   **Synthetic Vignette Gold Standard:** A set of 200 "conflict vignettes" curated by medical professionals where standard guidelines (e.g., AHA vs. KDIGO) provide contradictory treatment paths.

**6. Evaluation Metrics**
*   **Contraindication Recall:** The percentage of known medical contradictions (pre-identified by physicians) successfully flagged by the system.
*   **Safety Score:** A Likert scale (1-5) rating by board-certified physicians assessing the clinical safety of the final recommendation.
*   **Conflict Resolution Accuracy:** Whether the agent correctly prioritized the more life-threatening condition in a forced-choice scenario.

**7. Baselines**
*   **Zero-Shot GPT-4o:** A single prompt asking for a treatment plan for the multi-morbid patient.
*   **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) GPT-4o:** A single agent prompted to "think step-by-step" about the contradictions before providing an answer.
*   **Standard Multi-Agent Consensus:** A group of agents that are prompted to "collaborate" rather than "debate" (seeking immediate agreement).

**8. Ablations**
*   **No Role-Play:** Multi-agent setup where agents are not assigned specific specialties but are simply "Medical Assistants."
*   **No Debate:** Agents provide independent assessments to the Synthesizer without seeing each other's work (Parallel voting).
*   **Context Length Control:** Providing the single-agent baseline with the same "debate history" generated by the agents to see if the benefit comes from the structure or simply the increased computation/tokens.

**9. Failure Modes and Feasibility Risks**
*   **Deadlock:** The agents may fail to reach a consensus, leading the Synthesizer to produce a vague or non-actionable "consult a human" response.
*   **Hyper-Specialization Bias:** An agent might become so focused on its specialty that it ignores obvious, high-priority systemic risks (e.g., a nephrologist ignoring a clear cardiac arrest).
*   **Computational Cost:** Multi-agent debates significantly increase token usage and latency, which may be impractical for real-time emergency room triage.
*   **Feasibility:** Requires access to high-reasoning models (GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) to ensure the "debate" is substantive rather than repetitive.
