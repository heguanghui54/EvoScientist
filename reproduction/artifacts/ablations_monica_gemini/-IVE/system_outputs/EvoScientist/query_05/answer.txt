Title: Structured Critical Literature Review Generation via Hierarchical
Contradiction-Aware Synthesis---

Problem
Current automated literature review systems (LLM-based or
retrieval-augmented) produce fluent but shallow summaries — they list findings
paper-by-paper, avoid conflicting results, and fail to organize knowledge into
the canonical sub-topics, debates, and open questions a human reviewer would
surface. No existing system explicitly models contradictions across papers or
generates a structured review with sections like "consensus findings,"
"conflicting evidence," and "methodological gaps."

Hypothesis
A hierarchical, contradiction-aware pipeline that (1) retrieves and
clusters papers by sub-topic, (2) extracts claim-level triples (method, finding,
direction of effect), and (3) explicitly identifies and resolves or flags
contradictions, will produce literature reviews that are rated by domain experts
as more organized, more critical, and more useful than retrieval-augmented
baselines that only summarize per-paper or per-topic.

Method
Stage1 — Hierarchical topic structuring. Given a query (e.g.
"chain-of-thought reasoning"), query a paper corpus (e.g. S2ORC / Semantic
Scholar) and cluster retrieved abstracts using a hierarchical embedding +
LLM-labeling pipeline (HDBSCAN on SBERT embeddings, then GPT-4o-mini assigns
canonical section headings).

Stage2 — Claim extraction and contradiction detection. For each paper, extract
structured claim triples (entity, predicate, direction/sign) via a fine-tuned
DeBERTa-v3 or prompted LLM. Within each sub-topic cluster, compare claim triples
for directional mismatches (e.g. "CoT improves accuracy on GSM8K" vs "CoT does
not improve accuracy on GSM8K"). Flag contradictions with supporting citations
on both sides.

Stage3 — Contradiction-aware synthesis. Generate the review section-by-section
using a controlled generation loop: (a) draft a section from the consensus
claims, (b) conditionally append a "conflicting evidence" sub-section if
contradictions were detected, (c) append an "open questions" sub-section
identifying gaps where evidence is absent or contradictory.

Stage4 — Citation grounding and fact verification. Each generated claim is
back-linked to the original source passage. A lightweight fact-verification step
(NLI-based, e.g. TrueTeacher) verifies whether the generated claim is entailed
by the cited passage. Unverifiable claims are removed or softened.

Dataset / Benchmark- Corpus: Semantic Scholar Open Research Corpus (S2ORC) — ~8M
papers, with full text when available. Restrict to4-5 well-surveyed domains
(e.g., LLM reasoning, protein folding, adversarial robustness, representation
learning, NLP for low-resource languages).

• Gold-standard reviews: For each domain, collect2-3 human-written survey
papers (e.g. from arXiv, JMLR, ACL anthology). Segment into sections as
ground-truth structure.
• Review quality evaluation set: Construct50 query-review pairs per domain,
each with3 expert ratings along organization, coverage, critical depth, and
usefulness (1–5 Likert).

Evaluation Metrics| Dimension | Metric | How measured |

|---|---|---| | Structure | Section overlap (ROUGE-L / Jaccard on section
headings) | Against human survey TOC | | Coverage | Recall of cited papers from
human survey reference list | Macro-averaged per section | | Critical depth |
Contradiction recall — fraction of known conflicting findings surfaced |
Hand-labeled conflict set | | Faithfulness | % of claims entailed by cited

Expert rating (1–5) on organization, usefulness | Blind rating by3 domain

Baselines1. Naive RAG — Retrieve top-20 papers, concatenate abstracts, prompt
GPT-4o to write a review.

2 Per-paper summarization — Summarize each paper individually, then concatenate
under LLM-generated headings (Liu et al.,2024-style).
3 Topic-cluster summarization — Cluster papers by topic, summarize cluster,
then synthesize (similar to Scim,2024).
4 Ours w/o contradiction detection — Same pipeline but skip Stage2
contradiction flagging (ablate the core mechanism).

Ablations- A1: Remove hierarchical clustering → flat topic list (tests whether
hierarchy improves organization).

• A2: Remove fact-verification filter (tests whether filtering reduces
hallucination at the cost of coverage).
• A3: Replace GPT-4o claim extraction with fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3 (tests whether
a smaller model suffices).
• A4: Remove contradiction-aware sub-section generation → always write
consensus-only text (tests the value of explicit contradiction flagging vs.
implicit handling).

Expected Failure Modes1. Contradictions are rare or subtle. Most papers in a
well-matured field may agree on fundamentals, leaving little for the
contradiction module to surface. Mitigation: evaluate on at least one rapidly
evolving domain (e.g., LLM reasoning post-2023) where disagreements are common.

2 Claim extraction noise propagates. A false-positive contradiction (two claims
that appear contradictory but differ in experimental setup) degrades the
review. Mitigation: require supporting citations on both sides; add a
confidence threshold for contradiction flagging.
3 Expert raters prefer concise over critical. Human readers may rate a fluent,
uncontroversial summary higher than one that honestly reports messy
disagreements. Mitigation: structure expert evaluation to score critical
depth and usefulness separately from fluency and organization.
4 Structure diversity across human surveys. Two human surveys on the same topic
often use different section taxonomies, making section-overlap metrics noisy.
Mitigation: use macro-averaged ROUGE-L and report variance; also report
expert agreement (Fleiss' κ) on organization.
5 Domain shift. A pipeline tuned on NLP papers may fail for biomedical or
physics literature where citation practices and claim phrasing differ.
Mitigation: hold out one domain for final evaluation only, to measure
generalization.
