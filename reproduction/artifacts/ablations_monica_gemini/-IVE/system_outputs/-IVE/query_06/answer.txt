Multi-Source Phonetic Bootstrapping via Cross-Lingual Articulatory Priors for
Low-Resource Speech Recognition### TitleArticulatory-Constrained Multi-View
Representation Learning for Low-Resource Automatic Speech Recognition

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ProblemState-of-the-art automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems rely on
hundreds to thousands of hours of transcribed speech. For the vast majority of
the world's ~7,000 languages — many of which have fewer than10 hours of
transcribed data — these approaches fail. Existing low-resource methods fall
into three camps, each with a persistent limitation:

 1 Cross-lingual transfer (fine-tuning a multilingual pre-trained encoder like
   wav2vec2.0 or HuBERT onto a target language) — works only when the target
   language is well-represented in pre-training data; otherwise, phone-set
   mismatch and acoustic-phonetic divergence cause catastrophic forgetting
   during fine-tuning.
 2 Phonetic decoupling (using articulatory features like place/manner of
   articulation as intermediate representations) — improves interpretability but
   requires expert-annotated articulatory feature tables per language, which do
   not exist for most low-resource languages.
 3 Self-supervised speech representations — capture rich acoustic structure but
   lack explicit phone-level grounding, causing poor frame-to-phone alignment in
   zero-shot settings.

We address a specific subproblem: Can we build a phone-level ASR system for a
target language with <5 hours of transcribed speech, using only a small
articulatory feature matrix from a phonetically-related source language, without
requiring expert annotation in the target language?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HypothesisArticulatory priors — place, manner, and voicing feature vectors —
extracted from a phonologically related high-resource source language provide a
sufficiently universal phonetic constraint space that, when combined with a
multi-view contrastive objective between acoustic and articulatory embeddings,
enables phone-level transcription in an unseen low-resource target language
without any target-language articulatory annotations.
