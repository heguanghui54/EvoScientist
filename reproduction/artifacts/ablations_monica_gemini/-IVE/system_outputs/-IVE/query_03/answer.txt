Leveraging Generative Verifiers with Calibrated Uncertainty for Automated LLM
Evaluation## ProblemAutomated evaluation of Large Language Model outputs remains
   a critical bottleneck in LLM development. Current approaches fall into two
 camps: reference-based metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) that correlate poorly
with human judgment for open-ended generation, and LLM-as-judge methods that use
 a separate evaluator model to score outputs. The latter has shown promise but
 suffers from systematic biases—position bias, verbosity bias, self-enhancement
 bias, and poor calibration on out-of-distribution tasks. These accuracy issues
    are particularly acute for abstractive reasoning, creative writing, and
 instruction-following where there is no single ground-truth answer. Prior work
 treats the evaluator as a static scorer rather than as a reasoning agent that
can surface its own uncertainty; consequently, users have no signal for when an
                         automated score is unreliable.

HypothesisA generative verifier that produces both a categorical judgment and an
explicit confidence estimate—derived from structured reasoning traces over
multiple sampled evaluations—will achieve significantly higher agreement with
expert human raters than static LLM-as-judge baselines. Specifically, we
hypothesize that (1) aggregating multiple stochastic evaluation passes with a
principled confidence aggregation mechanism reduces per-instance scoring
variance, and (2) the resulting confidence scores are well-calibrated and can be
used to selectively defer to human evaluation when the verifier is uncertain,
lifting overall system-level accuracy.

MethodWe propose GVC-Eval (Generative Verifier with Calibrated Evaluation), a
two-stage framework:

Stage1 — Structured Scoring with Chain-of-Thought. For each candidate output,
the verifier LLM generates (k) independent evaluation traces using a structured
rubric prompt that breaks scoring into atomic criteria (relevance, factual
consistency, coherence, instruction adherence). Each trace produces a1–5 score
per criterion plus a natural language justification.
