Breaking the Performance Ceiling of Small Models via Multi-Scale Representation
Recycling## ProblemLarge language models (100B+ parameters) continue to improve
   predictably with scale under the Chinchin scaling laws, but models under1B
   parameters exhibit a pronounced performance ceiling: after approximately7B
  tokens of training, validation loss plateaus while downstream task accuracy
 saturates well below the levels predicted by extrapolating scaling curves from
   larger models [1,2]. This "small-model ceiling" manifests as a systematic
  underutilisation of capacity — small models fail to compress their training
 distribution beyond a certain point regardless of additional compute, data, or
training steps. The root cause is not capacity (a350M-parameter transformer has
 sufficient representational degrees of freedom), but rather that optimisation
  dynamics at small scale produce representations that are redundant, collapse
 along dominant principal components, and fail to capture long-tail patterns in
                                 the data [3].

Existing approaches — knowledge distillation from larger teachers [4], weight
pruning and quantisation [5], and architectural search for efficient
transformers [6] — treat the ceiling as a compression problem post-training.
They do not address the fundamental issue: the training process itself converges
to a poor local optimum where representations are under-specialised. A method
that directly intervenes on the representation learning trajectory rather than
compressing a converged model is needed.

HypothesisSmall models plateau because the gradient signal during training
becomes increasingly uniform across tokens and layers, collapsing the
representation manifold. We hypothesise that multi-scale representation
recycling — periodically injecting frozen, higher-quality representations from a
concurrently trained auxiliary module as structured regularisation targets —
prevents manifold collapse and forces the main model to maintain diverse,
composable features. Specifically, if an auxiliary model operating at a
different representational scale (wider or deeper in one critical dimension) is
trained jointly but constrains the main model's hidden states through a
cosine-similarity dispersion loss, the main model's effective rank of hidden
representations will remain above a critical threshold throughout training,
delaying the ceiling by at least a factor of three in training tokens.

The mechanism is not knowledge distillation (the auxiliary is not a converged
teacher) but rather structured perturbation of the loss landscape: the auxiliary
provides a moving target that prevents the main model from settling into a
low-rank attractor.

MethodWe propose a dual-model framework with three components.

Main model (M). A standard decoder-only transformer of350M parameters (12
layers, hidden1024,16 heads). Trained from scratch on the full training set.

Scale-shifted auxiliary (A). A structurally different transformer that shares
the vocabulary and embedding layer with M but differs along one architectural
axis: it has the same hidden dimension but twice the depth (24 layers) and half
the heads per layer (8 heads). This design ensures A operates on the same
token-level representation space as M but with different representational priors
— deeper composition, narrower attention. A is trained jointly with M on the
same data stream but receives its own optimiser state and learning rate
schedule. Critically, A is NOT a teacher: its representations are not converged
or fixed, and it is not used at inference time.

Dispersion regularisation. At each training step, for a random subset of50% of
layers in M, we extract the hidden state before the FFN at position h_{M}^{(l)}
and the corresponding hidden state from A at the closest aligned layer
h_{A}^{(l')} (linear mapping: l' = floor(l *24/12)). We compute a
centre-stopping cosine dispersion loss:


 L_disp = E[ (cos(h_M^{(l)}, h_A^{(l')}) - c_l)^2 ]


where c_l is a per-layer target correlation coefficient, linearly annealed
from0.9 at step0 to0.5 at step100k then held constant. The effect is
straightforward: early training encourages A's representations to pull M's
representations into a similar region (high correlation), but as training
progresses, the target correlation drops, forcing M to diverge from A and
explore a broader manifold. The loss coefficient alpha_disp is set to0.1
relative to the primary language modelling loss.

Final objective. Each training step optimises:


 L = L_lm(M) + L_lm(A) + alpha_disp * L_disp```

 Both models receive their own LM loss; the dispersion loss is symmetric and
 differentiable through both models. At inference, A is discarded entirely —
 only M is deployed, with no overhead.

 ## Dataset / Benchmark**Training data.** C4 (Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus)
 English subset, filtered to sequences of2048 tokens. We use50B tokens total,
 which is approximately7x the typical compute-optimal budget for a350M model
 (≈7B tokens under Chinchilla scaling), ensuring we operate deep into the
 ceiling regime.

 **Evaluation benchmarks.** We measure ceiling effects across four axes:
 - **Language modelling**: validation perplexity on held-out C4 slices
 at1B,5B,10B,25B, and50B training tokens- **Reasoning**: ARC-Challenge,
 ARC-Easy (accuracy)
 - **Factual knowledge**: MMLU (5-shot accuracy)
 - **Token-level understanding**: SuperGLUE (BoolQ, CB, COPA, RTE, WiC, WSC)

 All evaluations are performed every5,000 training steps (≈1B tokens) to
 produce training curves, not just final numbers.

 ## Evaluation Metrics| Metric | What It Measures | Target |
 |--------|-----------------|--------|
 | Validation perplexity gap | How far M is from the ideal
 Chinchilla-extrapolated PPL for its size | <0.5 nats gap at50B tokens |
 | Effective rank of hidden states | Manifold collapse (SVD entropy of hidden
 states across batches) | >80% of maximum rank throughout training |
 | Downstream accuracy ceiling slope | Accuracy change in the final25B tokens |
 Positive slope (not flat) |
 | Cross-layer representational similarity (CKA) | Whether layers are
 collapsing into each other | CKA decreasing, not increasing, after15B tokens |
 | Inference overhead | FLOPs and memory vs vanilla350M (should be zero after
 training) | Identical |

 ## Baselines| Baseline | Description | Why |
 |----------|-------------|-----|
 | **Vanilla350M** | Standard transformer trained without A or dispersion loss
 | Direct ablation of method |
 | **Knowledge distillation (KD)** | Teacher-forcing from a pretrained1.5B
 model (frozen) throughout training [4] | Tests whether a static teacher works
 better than dynamic A |
 | **DeepM reverse** | Swap roles: M as auxiliary, A as main (train A, discard
 M) | Controls for whether extra FLOPs alone explain gains |
 | **Co-training w/o dispersion** | Train M and A jointly with only `L_lm`, no
 `L_disp` | Isolates the effect of dispersion regularisation from co-training |
 | **Stochastic depth baseline** | M with50% stochastic depth [7] (another
 regulariser that prevents collapse) | Compares to an established regulariser
 for representation diversity |

 All baselines use the same data schedule, token budget, and evaluation
 cadence. Each is run with3 random seeds.
