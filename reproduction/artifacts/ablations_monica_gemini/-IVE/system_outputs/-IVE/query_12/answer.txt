Factual Consistency via Latent Source-Space Alignment in Large Language Models##
 TitleLatent Source-Space Alignment: Grounding LLM Generations Through Internal
                            Representation Steering

ProblemLarge Language Models generate fluent text that frequently contradicts
verifiable facts — a phenomenon spanning entity hallucinations, temporal
inconsistencies, and invented references. Existing mitigation strategies fall
into three families, each with a fundamental limitation: (i) retrieval-augmented
generation (RAG) conditions on external passages but cannot prevent the model
from ignoring or overriding retrieved content during decoding; (ii) prompt-based
methods (e.g. chain-of-thought with fact-checking instructions) are brittle
under distribution shift and offer no guarantees; (iii) fine-tuning on factual
corpora improves calibration on in-distribution topics but degrades on long-tail
queries and does not generalize to unseen domains. The core unsolved problem is
that the model's internal representations lack a mechanism to commit to a
verified source-state before generation proceeds — there is no architectural
induction that forces the decoded token distribution to remain faithful to
grounded premises.

HypothesisH1 (primary): Steering the hidden-state trajectory of the model toward
a verified source region — computed by constraining the attention distribution
over a retrieved passage to top-k supporting spans before each decoding step —
will reduce factual errors by at least30% relative to unsteered generation on
multiple-choice and free-form factuality benchmarks, without degrading fluency
beyond a1-point drop in perplexity.

H2 (secondary): The effectiveness of source-space steering is mediated by the
alignment gap between the model's internal representation of the query and its
representation of the supporting passage. When this gap falls below a learned
threshold, steering is unnecessary and may harm generation quality.

MethodWe propose Latent Source-Space Alignment (LSSA), a two-stage method that
operates at inference time without retraining the base model.

Stage1 — Passage Encoding and Span Selection. For each generation step, the
method begins with a retrieved or provided source passage. A lightweight
span-scoring module (a single transformer layer with a linear projection head,
trained on5K annotated factual-consistency pairs) computes a factuality score
for each contiguous span of up to64 tokens. Spans scoring above a threshold
(calibrated per-domain via a held-out validation set) form a verified source
region in the model's key-value cache.

Stage2 — Representation Steering. At each decoding layer ℓ, let hℓ be the hidden
state before the feed-forward sublayer. The method computes a steering vector sℓ
as the mean of the attention outputs attending to the verified source region.
The final hidden state is a convex combination: hᵢℓ = (1 − α**·w**ℓ) · hℓ +
α**·w**ℓ · sℓ, where α is a global hyperparameter and wℓ ∈ [0,1] is a
layer-specific gating weight produced by a learned policy network conditioned on
the alignment gap (H2). The policy network is trained via a small
reinforcement-learning loop (PPO, ~2K environment steps) with a reward that
combines factual accuracy and n-gram overlap with the reference.

This method requires no fine-tuning of the base LLM — only the span-scoring
module (Stage1) and the policy network (Stage2) must be trained, totaling
approximately15M parameters for a7B-base setup.

Dataset and BenchmarkWe evaluate across three factuality benchmarks:

 1 TruthfulQA (multiple-choice) —817 questions spanning common misconceptions
   and false beliefs. Primary metric: MC1 accuracy (single-true accuracy).
 2 FActScore (free-form generation) —500 Wikipedia biographies, each fact
   decomposed and verified. Primary metric: percentage of atomic facts
   supported.
 3 FreshQA (temporal factuality) —600 questions requiring up-to-date knowledge,
   with time-sensitive answers. Primary metric: accuracy with strict temporal
   grounding.
 4 SelfCheckGPT (internal consistency) —250 Wiki-style passages from GPT-3 with
   per-sentence factuality annotations. Primary metric: sentence-level F1 for
   consistency.

All benchmarks are English. We use GPTNeoX-20B as the base model for the main
experiments and scale to LLaMA-2-70B for the largest configuration.

Evaluation Metrics- Factual Accuracy (FA): Micro-averaged proportion of correct
atomic facts, averaged across all benchmarks. This is the primary metric.

 • Source Faithfulness (SF): Decomposable entailment score (using an
   off-the-shelf NLI model — DeBERTa-large-MNLI) measuring whether the
   generation is entailed by the provided source passage. Macro-averaged across
   benchmarks.
 • Perplexity (PPL): On the WikiText-103 validation set, to track fluency
   degradation.
 • Response Length Ratio (RLR): Ratio of generated tokens to reference tokens,
   to detect verbose hedging as a confound.
 • Steering Intervention Rate (SIR): Fraction of decoding steps where α·wℓ
   >0.05, measuring how frequently the steering mechanism activates.

BaselinesWe compare against six methods spanning the three existing mitigation
families:

 1 Standard greedy decoding (no intervention).
 2 RAG with top-1 passage (DPR retriever + standard autoregressive generation).
 3 RAG + self-consistency voting (Karpukhin et al.,2020 — sample5 generations,
   vote on factuality).
 4 Constitutional AI-style prompting (Bai et al.,2022 — "Generate only
   statements that are supported by the provided context" appended to the
   prompt).
 5 DoLa (Chuang et al.,2023 — contrastive decoding by contrasting early vs late
   layers on factuality).
 6 Inference-time contrastive steering (Li et al.,2024 — subtract "unfaithful"
   direction from hidden states, using a precomputed probe).

All baselines use the same base model (GPTNeoX-20B) and the same retriever (DPR)
where retrieval is applicable.

AblationsWe run four ablations to isolate the contribution of each component:

 1 -SpanSelection: Remove the span-scoring module; use the full retrieved
   passage as the verified source region. Tests whether span-level granularity
   matters.
 2 -AlignmentGate: Remove the learned gating policy (set wℓ =1 for all layers).
   Tests whether layer-specific adjustment is necessary.
 3 -Retrieval: Replace retrieved passages with the model's own generated
   preamble (zero-shot fact generation). Tests whether external grounding is
   required or whether the steering mechanism can exploit internally generated
   context.
 4 -Steering: Set α =0 across all steps. Reduces to the base model; establishes
   the upper bound of fluency with the lower bound of factuality.

Expected Failure Modes1. Soft source overfitting. The steering mechanism may
bias the model toward the lexical form of the source passage rather than its
semantic content, producing generations that are faithful to the source but
factually incorrect when the source itself is wrong. This is partially
controlled by using multiple benchmarks with independent ground truth, but
adversarial retrieval noise remains a risk.

 2 Alignment gap collapse. The policy network may learn to set wℓ near zero for
   all layers on certain domains, effectively disabling the intervention. This
   would produce null results indistinguishable from the base model. We monitor
   SIR per-domain to detect this.
 3 Layer-wise interference. Steering in middle layers may disrupt syntactic
   planning while late-layer steering may be too late to affect token choice.
   The learned gating may not resolve this if the reward landscape is flat. We
   will visualize layer-specific steering magnitudes post-hoc.
 4 Domain-dependent threshold sensitivity. The span-selection threshold
   calibrated on the held-out validation set may generalize poorly to benchmarks
   with different fact densities (e.g., FreshQA's temporal questions vs.
   TruthfulQA's misconception questions). We report per-benchmark results to
   expose this.
 5 Metric gaming. The NLI-based Source Faithfulness metric could be gamed by
   short, vague generations that are technically entailed by the source but omit
   specific claims. We mitigate by also reporting Factual Accuracy, which
   requires explicit factual claims.

Limitations1. Computational overhead. The LSSA method requires a forward pass
for span scoring and a modified decoding loop with per-layer steering,
approximately2.3× the inference cost of standard greedy decoding on a single
A100. This may be prohibitive for latency-sensitive applications.

 2 Dependence on retrieval quality. The method assumes a relevant source passage
   is available. For open-domain generation where no passage is retrieved or the
   retrieval pool is incomplete, the method degrades to the base model. The
   -Retrieval ablation partially tests this scenario, but the degradation curve
   under retrieval sparsity is not characterized.
 3 English-only evaluation. All benchmarks are English. The span-scoring module
   and alignment policy are trained on English data only, and there is no
   guarantee that the learned representations transfer to other languages with
   different factuality norms and syntactic structures.
 4 Static source assumption. The method treats the source passage as fixed
   before generation begins. For multi-turn or interactive settings where new
   evidence arrives mid-generation, the steering region would need to be
   recomputed, which the current design does not support.
 5 No formal correctness guarantee. Unlike constrained decoding methods that
   enforce a grammar, LSSA is a soft steering mechanism. It reduces the
   probability of factual errors but cannot eliminate them. For high-stakes
   domains requiring verifiable correctness (medical, legal), this method should
   be combined with post-hoc verification rather than relied upon alone.
 6 No validation-evolution memory. This proposal is generated without reference
   to a bank of prior failed directions, execution failures, or reusable
   rejection criteria. Consequently, some proposed design choices (e.g.,
   layer-specific gating, span-level scoring) may replicate failure patterns
   already documented in prior work. Without cross-run validation memory, the
   method is at increased risk of rediscovering known negative results during
   implementation.
