TitleCross-lingual Subnet Distillation: Efficient Adaptation of Multilingual NMT
via Lightweight Adapter Routing for Extreme Low-Resource Languages

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ProblemMultilingual neural machine translation (MNMT) models such as M2M-100 and
NLLB cover hundreds of language pairs but degrade sharply on extreme
low-resource languages (training corpus <100K sentence pairs). Two common
approaches — full fine-tuning and language-family adapter modules — suffer from
either catastrophic forgetting of high-resource languages or parameter
inefficiency when scaling to dozens of low-resource pairs. Existing
adapter-based methods typically assign one adapter per language or per language
pair, which does not exploit cross-lingual transfer between typologically
related low-resource languages that share structural patterns. The core problem
is: how can we share a small set of adapter parameters across related
low-resource languages without hand-crafted language groupings or prior
knowledge about which languages benefit from sharing?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HypothesisA shared pool of lightweight adapter modules combined with a learned
routing mechanism can automatically discover beneficial parameter-sharing
patterns among extreme low-resource languages. Specifically:

 • A small set of cross-lingual subnets (e.g.,4–8 adapter modules per
   Transformer layer) is sufficient to cover the syntactic and morphological
   variation across a diverse set of low-resource languages.
 • A gating network trained jointly with the adapters can route each language's
   hidden states through the appropriate subnets, enabling positive transfer
   while preventing interference.
 • This approach yields BLEU/COMET gains of ≥2.0 points over per-language
   adapters and ≥1.5 points over language-family adapters on the lowest-resource
   quartile of FLORES-200, with <5% additional parameters over the base MNMT
   model.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MethodThree-stage pipeline:

 1 Base model & frozen backbone. Start from a pretrained MNMT encoder-decoder
   (e.g., NLLB-600M). Freeze all backbone parameters. Insert a small set of K
   learnable adapter subnets per Transformer layer (each subnet =
   down-projection → ReLU → up-projection, bottleneck dimension d =64). This
   follows the standard bottleneck adapter architecture (Houlsby et al.,2019)
   but with multiple independent copies per layer.
 2 Learned router. For each language l in the set of target low-resource
   languages, a lightweight gating network takes the hidden state h at each
   layer and outputs a soft distribution over the K subnets:

 • Router = single linear layer with softmax: g(h) = softmax(W_r · h), output
   dimension K.
 • The final adapter output is the weighted combination of all K subnets: Σ_k
   g_k(h) · subnet_k(h).
 • Router parameters are shared across all low-resource languages but
   conditioned on the current language via a learned language embedding.

 3 Training. Train adapters + router jointly on a curated set of ~20 extreme
   low-resource languages (each with5K–100K sentence pairs). Use a
   temperature-annealed version of the routing softmax to encourage
   specialization over time. Include a small auxiliary loss that penalizes
   entropy of the routing distribution to incentivize each language to select a
   compact subset of subnets.

During inference for a held-out low-resource language, the router automatically
selects the appropriate mixture of subnets without any manual grouping.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dataset / Benchmark- Training: A subset of the FLORES-200 dataset covering20
extreme low-resource languages (e.g., Acehnese, Balinese, Bambara, Central
Kurdish, Crimean Tatar, Dholuo, Eastern Mari, Fijian, Guarani, Ilocano, Kamba,
Kashmiri, Kikuyu, Kinyarwanda, Kyrgyz, Lombard, Madurese, Maori, Minangkabau,
Nuer), paired with English. Each pair has between5K and100K parallel sentences
from the NLLB corpus.

 • Evaluation: FLORES-200 devtest for the same20 languages (translation into
   English and from English). Primary: spBLEU (sacrébleu tokenized) and COMET-22
   (wmt22-comet-da). Secondary: chrF++.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Baselines1. NLLB-600M (frozen backbone + no adapters): Zero-shot baseline —
simply evaluate the pretrained model without any additional training. Measures
the gap this proposal aims to close.

 2 Per-language adapters (NLLB +1 adapter/lang): One dedicated bottleneck
   adapter per low-resource language, no sharing. Upper bound on parameter
   count; lower bound on cross-lingual transfer.
 3 Language-family adapters (grouped by NLLB taxonomy): One adapter per
   predefined language family (e.g., Austronesian, Niger-Congo, Turkic).
   Represents the best static-grouping baseline.
 4 Full fine-tuning of NLLB on the20-language corpus: Upper bound on
   per-language performance but at high forgetting risk. Included to quantify
   the forgetting-adaptation trade-off.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Expected Failure Modes1. Collapse to uniform routing. If the router assigns
equal weight to all subnets for every language, the method degenerates into a
single larger adapter. Mitigation: the entropy penalty explicitly penalizes
this; monitor router entropy as a diagnostic.

 2 Low-resource languages too dissimilar. If the20 languages are so
   typologically diverse that no beneficial sharing exists, per-language
   adapters may outperform the shared-pool approach. Mitigation: stratify the
   language set to include at least two pairs of closely related languages
   (e.g., Madurese/Indonesian, Kikuyu/Kamba) to maximize chance of detectable
   transfer.
 3 Instability in routing during early training. Random initial routing may
   cause noisy gradients that prevent convergence. Mitigation: use a warmup
   phase where routing is uniform (temperature high), then anneal.
 4 Catastrophic forgetting of high-resource languages. Even with a frozen
   backbone, adapter outputs may drift. Mitigation: evaluate BLEU on French and
   Chinese after training; if drop exceeds0.5 BLEU, add a small replay buffer of
   high-resource pairs.
 5 Marginal gains on very small datasets (<10K pairs). With extremely limited
   data, even parameter sharing may not help; the bottleneck dimension may be
   too large relative to the training signal. Mitigation: include a baseline
   with d =32 and consider whether pretrained word-level alignments (e.g.,
   LASER) could augment the signal.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Limitations1. English-centric. Both training and evaluation are
English⇄low-resource. The method's generality to low-resource⇄low-resource
translation is untested.

 2 Bottleneck adapter capacity. Adapters are fundamentally low-rank
   approximations; for morphologically complex languages, a rank-64 bottleneck
   may underspecify certain inflectional phenomena. Character-level or
   morpheme-level decoding is not explored here.
 3 Router training cost. Jointly training the gating network introduces
   additional training overhead (~1.5× per-step cost vs. per-language adapters)
   and may require careful hyperparameter tuning across20 languages
   simultaneously.
 4 No explicit orthographic or phonological features. The router conditions only
   on pretrained language embeddings, not on typological features (e.g., word
   order, case marking) that could guide sharing. This is a deliberate design
   choice to avoid human priors, but it may leave gains on the table.
 5 Single backbone scale (600M parameters). Whether the findings transfer to
   larger (3.3B) or smaller (300M) backbones is unknown. The optimal K and d
   likely scale with model capacity.
 6 No iterative back-translation or monolingual data. The proposal uses only
   parallel data. Incorporating monolingual data (back-translation,
   self-training) is a natural extension but is outside the scope of this
   method's core contribution.
