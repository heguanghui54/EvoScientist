Knowledge Injection in Domain-Specific LLMs: A Curriculum-Grounded Parameter
Integration Framework### TitleCurriculum-Guided Parameter Integration for Robust
Knowledge Injection in Domain-Specific Large Language Models

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ProblemInjecting structured, domain-specific knowledge into pretrained LLMs
faces three interconnected challenges not adequately solved by existing methods:

 1 Catastrophic forgetting vs. insufficient integration — Fine-tuning on domain
   corpora either overwrites general capabilities (low retention of general
   knowledge) or fails to internalize domain knowledge beyond surface-level
   pattern matching.
 2 Granularity mismatch — Domain knowledge exists at multiple granularities
   (fact-level, concept-level, procedural/rule-level), yet current injection
   methods typically apply a single mechanism (continued pretraining, retrieval
   augmentation, or adapter tuning) that treats all knowledge types uniformly.
 3 Evaluation confound — Standard benchmarks measure isolated fact recall (e.g.,
   multiple-choice QA) but not the model's ability to reason compositionally
   with injected knowledge — e.g., combining two injected facts to answer a
   novel question. Reported gains may reflect memorization rather than genuine
   knowledge integration.

The core challenge is therefore not whether knowledge can be injected, but how
to inject knowledge at multiple granularities such that the model can flexibly
compose and reason with it while preserving general capabilities.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HypothesisStructuring domain knowledge into a curriculum of increasing
compositional complexity and injecting each level via a dedicated,
minimally-interfering parameterization will produce higher compositional
reasoning accuracy and lower general-knowledge degradation than either
monolithic fine-tuning or single-mechanism injection.

Specifically: (1) fact-level knowledge is best injected via lightweight
memory-augmented parameters (key-value adapters), (2) concept-level knowledge
via rank-constrained LoRA modules on mid-layer attention, and (3)
procedural/rule-level knowledge via sparse feed-forward network grafting.
Training these in a curriculum order (facts → concepts → rules) and freezing
each stage's parameters before introducing the next reduces interference and
improves compositional generalization.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MethodCIPI (Curriculum-grounded Injection with Parameter Isolation)

 1 Knowledge Decomposition Pipeline

 • Parse the domain corpus into three tiers via LLM-assisted extraction + human
   verification sampling:
 • Tier1 (Facts): atomic triples (entity, relation, entity) — e.g., (Drug X,
   treats, Disease Y) - Tier2 (Concepts): cluster-level representations linking
   related facts — e.g., mechanism-of-action clusters linking drugs, targets,
   and pathways - Tier3 (Procedures/Rules): conditional inference rules — e.g.,
   "If patient has condition A and biomarker B > threshold, then drug X is
   contraindicated"

 2 Three-Parameter Injection Modules

 • Fact Injector (Tier1): A small set of cross-attention key-value adapters
   inserted after each Transformer layer's self-attention. The keys index fact
   triples; the values project retrieved facts into the residual stream. No
   base-model weights are updated. Motivation: facts are lookup-intensive;
   retrieval-based injection suits this without touching general reasoning.
 • Concept Injector (Tier2): Low-rank adaptation (LoRA, r=8) applied to the
   value projections in the middle-third of layers (layers12–24 of a32-layer
   model). Trained on concept-grounded next-token prediction where concept
   labels are prepended as soft prompts. Motivation: concept abstraction
   benefits from distributed weight updates in layers associated with semantic
   composition.
 • Procedure Injector (Tier3): Sparse grafting — replace a small fraction (1–2%)
   of feed-forward network neurons in the last third of layers with
   domain-rule-specialized neurons initialized from the rule corpus. Trained
   with L0 sparsity regularization. Motivation: rules require deterministic
   branching; sparse FFN specialization preserves the bulk of general knowledge.

 3 Curriculum Schedule

 • Phase1: Train Fact Injector alone (freeze base + all other modules).1 epoch.
 • Phase2: Freeze Fact Injector. Train Concept Injector.1 epoch.
 • Phase3: Freeze all prior. Train Procedure Injector.1 epoch.
 • Phase4 (optional): Joint very-low-rate training (lr ×0.1) on mixed data to
   allow cross-module coordination.

 4 Inference

 • All three modules are active. The Fact Injector retrieves relevant facts, the
   Concept Injector provides contextual abstraction, and the Procedure Injector
   applies rule-based branching where triggered.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dataset / Benchmark- Domain: Biomedical (chosen for well-structured knowledge
bases and established benchmarks).

 • Training corpus: A curated set of ~200K biomedical documents spanning:
 • DrugBank (facts: drug-target-disease triples)
 • UMLS (concepts: semantic type clusters)
 • Clinical practice guidelines (rules: conditional treatment protocols)
 • Evaluation benchmarks:
 • Fact recall: BioQA (in-domain multiple-choice) and a held-out triple
   completion task (Tier1)
 • Concept reasoning: MedMCQA subset requiring multi-step inference (Tier2)
 • Rule application: a novel compositional benchmark constructed by composing
   two facts into a rule-required question (Tier3) — e.g., "Drug X treats
   Disease Y. Disease Y has symptom Z. Does Drug X alleviate Z?" requires fact
   lookup + rule application - General retention: MMLU (full), HellaSwag, and
   GSM8K — to measure forgetting- Base model: Llama-3-8B (Instruct).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Baselines1. Full fine-tune (FFT): Continued pretraining on the domain corpus
(all model weights). Represents maximum injection with minimum parameter
efficiency.

 2 LoRA-only (r=16): Single LoRA module on all attention projections, trained on
   the full corpus (no curriculum, no tier separation). Matches computational
   budget to CIPI.
 3 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): No weight updates — retrieve top-3
   facts from the knowledge base at each generation step. Represents the pure
   retrieval baseline.
 4 CIPI (uniform joint): Same three modules, but trained jointly (no curriculum)
   on all tiers simultaneously. Isolates the curriculum effect.
 5 CIPI (reverse order): Rules → Concepts → Facts curriculum. Isolates the
   ordering effect.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Expected Failure Modes1. Tier bleed: Fact Injector retrieves facts irrelevant to
the compositional question, misleading the Procedure Injector. Mitigation: train
a lightweight gating mechanism that down-weights retrieved facts when
concept-level reasoning is active.

 2 Curriculum lock-in: Early frozen modules prevent later modules from learning
   useful cross-tier interactions. Mitigation: Phase4 joint low-rate training is
   designed to address this; if it fails, relax freezing to allow gradual
   fine-tuning of early modules at very low learning rates.
 3 Sparse grafting underutilization: The L0-regularized FFN neurons collapse to
   near-zero activation, rendering the Procedure Injector inert. Mitigation:
   initialize grafted neurons with weights from rule-derived auxiliary training
   (not random) and schedule L0 regularization to activate only after a warmup
   phase.
 4 General knowledge degradation still exceeds5%: The cumulative number of
   updated parameters (~2–3% of total) may still drift general representations.
   Mitigation: add a KL-divergence regularization term between the base model's
   logits and CIPI's logits on a held-out general-domain corpus during all
   training phases.
 5 Compositional benchmark is too easy / too hard: A novel benchmark risks
   ceiling or floor effects. Mitigation: calibrate difficulty on a small pilot
   (N=100 examples) with human expert baseline accuracy. Iterate on question
   design to target50–70% human accuracy before full-scale evaluation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Limitations1. Knowledge decomposition cost: Extracting the three tiers from a
new domain requires significant human-in-the-loop validation (target:95%
precision on a5% sampled audit). This limits deployment speed in low-resource
domains.

 2 Modularity ceiling: Separating knowledge into three tiers assumes clean
   boundaries between facts, concepts, and rules. In practice, these boundaries
   are fuzzy — a single sentence may encode all three. The decomposition
   pipeline's errors propagate to the injection modules.
 3 Scale dependence: The curriculum and modular design is tested on8B
   parameters. At much larger scales (70B+), interference patterns and optimal
   tier definitions may shift qualitatively.
 4 Sequential dependency: The curriculum assumes that fact-level knowledge is a
   prerequisite for concept-level reasoning. If a domain requires top-down
   reasoning (rules constrain which facts are relevant), the current order may
   be suboptimal. The reverse-curriculum ablation tests this, but deployment
   would need to determine the correct order per domain.
 5 No lifelong learning evaluation: The proposal tests a one-shot injection
   scenario. Real-world knowledge bases evolve; CIPI's modular design is
   intended to support updating individual tiers without retraining, but this is
   not evaluated in the proposed experiments.
