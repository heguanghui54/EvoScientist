Title: Unified Audio Foundation Models: A Multi-Task, Multi-Domain Pretraining
FrameworkProblem: Audio understanding spans diverse tasks — speech recognition,
speaker diarization, music analysis, sound event detection, and paralinguistics
— each traditionally addressed by specialized architectures and training
recipes. Recent foundation models aim to unify these, but they either target a
single audio domain (e.g., speech only), or they scale a single architecture
across domains without principled handling of the fragmented label spaces,
variable-length temporal structure, and conflicting optimization objectives
inherent to multi-domain audio. The core challenge is: can one model, one
pretraining stage, and one finetuning protocol match or exceed specialist
performance across all major audio tasks simultaneously?

Hypothesis: A transformer encoder pretrained with a hybrid objective combining
masked audio modeling, contrastive alignment with paired text labels, and a
domain-adaptive loss weighting scheme will produce representations that
generalize across speech, music, and environmental sound tasks, achieving
competitive per-task performance without task-specific architectural
modifications.

Method:

 1 Architecture: A Conformer-style encoder (convolution-augmented transformer)
   with12 layers,768 hidden dim,8 attention heads, and a50 Hz framerate output.
   To handle variable-length input, we use a learned positional encoding scheme
   and a simple mean-pooling readout for utterance-level tasks. No task-specific
   output heads during pretraining — the shared representation is the only model
   body.
 2 Pretraining data (multi-domain, ~100k hours):

 • Speech: LibriLight (60k hours) + Common Voice (20k hours) + VoxPopuli (10k
   hours)
 • Music: MusicNet + MAESTRO + FMA-large (combined ~10k hours)
 • Environmental sound: AudioSet (6k hours) + FSD50K + ESC-50 Total ≈100k hours;
   all resampled to16 kHz, normalized to -25 dB LUFS.

 3 Pretraining objectives (three-term hybrid):

 • Masked Audio Modeling (MAM): Mask25% of time-frequency patches; predict
   normalized log-mel spectrogram reconstructions via MSE loss.
 • Contrastive Language-Audio Pretraining (CLAP): Align audio encoder outputs
   with paired textual descriptions using a symmetric InfoNCE loss, with a
   shared text encoder (frozen DistilBERT). This provides a zero-shot interface.
 • Domain-Adaptive Loss Weighting (DALW): Learn a scalar per domain
   (speech/music/environmental) that weights the three losses dynamically during
   pretraining, using uncertainty-based homoscedastic task weighting to prevent
   one domain from dominating.

 4 Finetuning protocol: For each downstream task, attach a small linear
   projection head (1-2 layers). Finetune the full model with a task-specific
   loss for10% of the pretraining steps at a5× lower learning rate. Report both
   finetuned and zero-shot results.

Benchmark & Datasets (6 tasks across3 domains):

 • Speech: LibriSpeech test-clean/test-other (ASR WER), SUPERB (phoneme
   recognition, speaker identification)
 • Music: MusicNet (note onset/offset/frame F1), MAESTRO (piano transcription)
 • Environmental sound: ESC-50 (zero-shot classification accuracy), AudioSet
   (sound event detection mAP)

Evaluation Metrics:

 • Per-task: WER (speech), F1 (music transcription), accuracy (environmental),
   mAP (AudioSet)
 • Aggregate: Average normalized rank across tasks (lower is better), with rank
   computed per task relative to published specialist baselines.
 • Zero-shot: CLAP-based linear probe accuracy on ESC-50, AudioSet.

Baselines:

 1 Specialist per task: wav2vec2.0 (speech), MT3 (music), AST (environmental
   sound)
 2 Unified but domain-only: HuBERT (speech only), CLAP (audio-text only)
 3 Full unified baselines: Whisper (speech only, large), WavLM (speech +
   non-speech), AudioMAE (masked autoencoder), HEAR challenge winnersAblations
   (the proposed model, varied systematically):

 • Remove MAM → only CLAP + DALW- Remove CLAP → only MAM + DALW- Remove DALW →
   uniform loss weighting- Remove pretraining entirely → train from scratch per
   task- Half the pretraining data (50k hours) — measure data scaling- Single
   domain pretraining → pretrain on speech only then finetune on all tasks
   (tests cross-domain transfer from one specialization)
 • ★ This experiment (-IDE): Substitute domain-adaptive loss weighting with
   equal weighting and remove the learned per-domain gating. No idea-direction
   evolution — no mining of past successful representations to inform the
   weighting scheme. The DALW gating parameters are set to fixed uniform values,
   and no memory of which domains proved "easy" or "hard" in prior rounds is
   retained. Loss weights are set to [1/3,1/3,1/3] throughout training.

Expected Failure Modes:

 • Domain interference: Speech, music, and environmental sounds have very
   different spectral structure. Joint training might degrade all three if the
   optimizer oscillates between conflicting gradient directions. The DALW
   ablation (-IDE) is expected to exacerbate this — uniform weighting may let
   the dominant domain (by data volume, e.g., speech) suppress the others.
 • CLAP collapse: If paired text descriptions are not available uniformly across
   domains (e.g., music captions are scarce), the contrastive loss may provide
   weak signal for music and environmental tasks.
 • Masked modeling mismatch: MAM reconstruction works well for speech
   (continuous, periodic) but may be less informative for transient events in
   environmental audio.
 • Zero-shot brittleness: CLAP-based zero-shot on ESC-50 may underperform simple
   supervised linear probes by10–20 points absolute.

Limitations:

 • The100k-hour pretraining corpus, while large, is an order of magnitude
   smaller than proprietary models (e.g., Whisper's680k hours, WavLM's94k hours
   on speech alone with significant filtering). Data scaling may plateau or not
   yet saturate.
 • We do not include generative tasks (text-to-speech, music generation, audio
   inpainting) — this is a representation-only model. Extending to generative
   finetuning would require architectural changes (e.g., a decoder).
 • The benchmark covers6 tasks; many important tasks are omitted (e.g., speaker
   diarization, emotion recognition, language identification, audio captioning).
   Generalization to those is not guaranteed.
 • Domain-adaptive loss weighting adds three learned scalar parameters, which is
   lightweight computationally but may still underfit per-domain dynamics. More
   sophisticated methods (gradient surgery, PCGrad) are not tested.
 • Computational cost: The Conformer-12L model at50 Hz framerate with100k hours
   of pretraining requires approximately4,096 GPU-hours on A100s, placing it out
   of reach for most academic groups without cluster access.
