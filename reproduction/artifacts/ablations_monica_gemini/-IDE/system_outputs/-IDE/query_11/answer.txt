Decoupling Emergent Capabilities in Large Language Models: A Mechanistic
Independence Framework### TitleDecoupling Emergent Capabilities in Large
Language Models: A Mechanistic Independence Framework

ProblemLarge language models exhibit a diverse and growing set of capabilities —
instruction following, multi-step reasoning, factual recall, code generation,
multilingual translation, and creative composition. The dominant training
paradigm (next-token prediction on a single mixed corpus) bundles all of these
into a single set of weights, making it impossible to answer a foundational
question: to what extent are these capabilities mechanistically separable?
Current alignment interventions (RLHF, DPO) and capability editing methods
(model patching, weight interpolation) implicitly assume at least partial
independence, yet we lack a principled framework to test that assumption.
Without understanding which capabilities are entangled and which are separable,
safety interventions risk collateral damage, fine-tuning risks catastrophic
forgetting of unrelated skills, and scaling offers no systematic path to
composing capabilities modularly.

HypothesisWe hypothesize that LLM capabilities form a structured independence
graph rather than a monolithic competence. Specifically:

 • Reasoning (multi-step deduction, math, logic) and factual recall (memorized
   knowledge) are substantially dissociable — they depend on partially
   non-overlapping circuit motifs and can be independently perturbed.
 • Instruction following and content generation quality are partially entangled
   — models use similar attention patterns to parse and execute instructions,
   but the factual correctness of the output can be separated from format
   compliance.
 • Syntactic fluency (grammar, style) is a near-independent substrate that
   persists under most perturbations, acting as a shared backbone rather than a
   capability-specific circuit.

These relationships can be quantified through a Capability Independence Score
(CIS) — the expected degradation in capability B given optimal intervention to
preserve capability A — and visualized as a directed graph where edge weights
correspond to conditional dependence strength.

MethodWe propose a three-phase intervention framework:

Phase1 — Capability Probes and Baselines. For each target capability (reasoning,
factual recall, instruction following, code generation, safety refusal, creative
generation), we construct a suite of held-out prompts that isolate the
capability with minimal confounds. We measure baseline performance on each suite
for a family of open-weight models (Pythia, LLaMA-2/3, Mistral, Qwen-2) at
multiple scales (1B–70B params). Each capability gets a scalar score derived
from an automated judge (LLM-as-evaluator with rubric) plus objective metrics
where available (pass@k for code, accuracy for math).

Phase2 — Targeted Interventions. We apply four classes of intervention, each
designed to perturb a single capability while leaving others untouched:

 1 Representation Steering. Identify steering vectors in the residual stream
   that maximally activate (or suppress) a capability-specific probe. Apply
   these vectors during generation and measure cross-capability transfer.
   Expected: strong intra-capability effect, varying cross-capability spillover.
 2 Circuit Ablation (activation patching). Use causal tracing (Wang et al.,2023)
   to localize attention heads and MLP neurons responsible for each capability.
   Patch those activations to corrupted runs and measure capability-specific
   degradation.
 3 Fine-Tuning on Capability-Specific Data. Continue training on data that
   exclusively exercises one capability (e.g.,50K math problems) and measure
   improvement in the target capability vs. regression in non-target
   capabilities.
 4 Weight Interpolations (model soup with capability-specific fine-tunes).
   Independently fine-tune copies on single-capability data, then linearly
   interpolate weights and measure whether capability gains compose additively
   or exhibit interference.

Phase3 — Independence Graph Construction. For each pair of capabilities (A, B),
compute the CIS:

▌ CIS(A→B) =1 − (Performance_B after optimal intervention targeting A) /
▌ (Baseline_Performance_B)

A CIS near0 means the intervention on A leaves B unaffected (strong
independence). A CIS near1 means B collapses when A is perturbed (strong
dependence). The pairwise matrix yields a directed graph where edges >0.3
indicate entanglement.

We then validate the graph by checking whether the intervention type (steering
vs. ablation vs. fine-tuning) produces consistent dependence patterns — if all
four methods agree on the graph structure, the independence claim is robust.
