 Proposal: DIVERSE — A Composite Framework for Evaluating Semantic and Lexical
    Diversity in Text Generation## ProblemAutoregressive language models are
      typically optimized for likelihood, which drives models toward safe,
     high-probability tokens. The result is text that is fluent but bland,
  repetitive, and lacking in lexical and semantic breadth. While diversity is
    widely recognized as a critical quality dimension alongside fluency and
            relevance, the field lacks a reliable way to measure it.

Existing metrics fall into two camps, both with major blind spots.

Lexical n-gram metrics (Distinct‑1/2, Self-BLEU, entropy) are cheap and
interpretable but fundamentally shallow. They are sensitive to sentence length,
conflate type variation with semantic breadth, and can be gamed — a model that
cycles through rare synonyms without changing meaning scores higher than one
that actually explores distinct content.

Semantic embedding metrics (sentential embedding distance, coverage in latent
space) address the surface-level problem but introduce their own: they depend on
the quality and bias of the embedding model, they are sensitive to the choice of
reference distribution, and there is no single agreed-upon aggregator that
correlates reliably with human diversity judgments.

No existing evaluation jointly conditions on three desiderata: (1) diversity of
tokens, (2) diversity of meaning, and (3) diversity of structural patterning
beyond bag-of-words properties. Furthermore, there is no standardised synthetic
benchmark where true diversity is known by construction, making it impossible to
calibrate metrics against ground truth.

HypothesisA composite metric that combines three complementary signals —
semantic dispersion in embedding space, compression-based novelty relative to a
reference corpus, and length-calibrated n-gram diversity — will correlate with
human judgments of diversity more strongly (Spearman ρ ≥0.80 on held-out
evaluation) than any single signal alone, and will be more robust to length
confounds and adversarial surface-level manipulation.

Method: DIVERSE FrameworkWe propose the Diversity Index Via Entropic
Representation and Semantic Expansion (DIVERSE) metric, defined as the weighted
arithmetic mean of three component scores:

Component A — Semantic Dispersion (SD)

Embed each generated text with a frozen sentence encoder (Sentence-T5-XXL).
Compute the mean pairwise cosine distance across all generated texts in a set.
Then compute the coverage radius: the minimum sphere in embedding space that
encloses a specified fraction (e.g.,95%) of the set's representations,
normalised by the same radius for a reference corpus of equivalent size. Higher
values indicate broader semantic coverage.

Component B — Compression-Based Novelty (CN)

Concatenate all generated texts into a single string. Compute the
gzip-compressed length ratio: (original_length - compressed_length) /
original_length. Separately compute the same ratio for the reference corpus. The
novelty score is the gap between the two ratios. Intuitively, if the generated
text compresses nearly as well as the reference, it contains little new
information; if it compresses poorly, it is more novel relative to what has been
seen.
