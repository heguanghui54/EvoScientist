Breaking the Performance Ceiling of Small Language Models via Multi-Source
Knowledge Consolidation## TitleCross-Parameter Knowledge Consolidation: Breaking
      the Performance Ceiling of Sub-1B Language Models Through Structured
                           Multi-Source Distillation

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ProblemSmall language models (<1B parameters) exhibit a persistent performance
ceiling: beyond a certain scale of training data and compute, returns on
standard metrics (perplexity, downstream task accuracy) sharply diminish. This
ceiling is widely attributed to capacity constraints, but evidence suggests that
the bottleneck is not raw parameter count alone — small models trained on
well-curated data (e.g., Phi-1, TinyLlama, Qwen-0.5B) show that architecture and
data quality matter at least as much as scale. The open question is: given a
fixed small-parameter budget, what training recipe most effectively closes the
gap with larger models?

Existing approaches fall into three camps, each with limitations:

 1 Static knowledge distillation (Hinton et al.,2015) — transfer from one large
   teacher to one small student at a single point in training. The student
   inherits the teacher's blind spots.
 2 Data filtering / deduplication (e.g., DCI, FineWeb, C4) — improves
   signal-to-noise ratio but does not create new knowledge; ceiling is the
   information content of the training corpus.
 3 Architecture search (e.g., SandwichNorm, ALiBi, GQA) — improves efficiency
   but has yielded diminishing returns over the past two generations of small
   models.

Hypothesis: The performance ceiling of small models is not fundamentally a
capacity problem but an optimization conflict problem. A single small model
cannot simultaneously absorb conflicting representations from diverse sources
(different teacher models, different data domains, different training
objectives). We hypothesize that a structured, staged, multi-source
consolidation procedure — where knowledge from multiple heterogeneous teachers
is decomposed into complementary sub-capabilities and consolidated sequentially
— can raise the ceiling by15–25% on composite benchmarks relative to standard
distillation baselines.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MethodWe propose Cross-Parameter Knowledge Consolidation (CPKC), a three-stage
training pipeline designed to resolve optimization conflicts in small-model
learning.
