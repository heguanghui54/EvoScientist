Research Proposal: Evaluating Diversity in Text Generation via Distributional
 Alignment Metrics## TitleDistributional Alignment for Evaluating Diversity in
              Neural Text Generation: A Benchmark and Metric Suite

ProblemCurrent text generation evaluation is dominated by quality-oriented
metrics (perplexity, BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore, GPT-as-judge) that primarily
measure how well a model matches a reference. Diversity — the property that a
model generates outputs spanning the full range of plausible alternatives rather
than collapsing to a narrow mode — remains poorly captured by these metrics.
High-quality but degenerate generators (e.g., beam-search-decoded models
producing near-identical outputs, or fine-tuned LLMs that over-anchor to
frequent patterns) score well on quality metrics yet fail in deployment where
output variety matters.

Existing diversity metrics such as distinct-n, self-BLEU, and perplexity of
generated sequences each have known blind spots: distinct-n rewards
surface-level token variation without semantic structure, self-BLEU conflates
fluency with diversity, and per-sequence perplexity is sensitive to length
artifacts. The field lacks a unified framework that (a) measures diversity at
the distributional level — comparing the model's output distribution to the true
data distribution — and (b) isolates diversity from quality in a principled way.

HypothesisDistributional alignment metrics — specifically, estimates of the
divergence between the model's generated text distribution and a reference
corpus distribution in a semantically meaningful embedding space — provide a
more reliable, interpretable, and quality-orthogonal measure of diversity than
surface-form heuristics. We hypothesize that:

 1 Embedding-based distributional divergence (e.g., sliced Wasserstein distance,
   maximum mean discrepancy, or kernelized Stein discrepancy computed over
   sentence embeddings) correlates with human judgments of output variety more
   strongly than distinct-n or self-BLEU.
 2 These metrics are approximately orthogonal to quality metrics — a model can
   be improved on distributional alignment without degrading BERTScore, and vice
   versa — meaning they capture a distinct axis of generation quality.
 3 A composite diversity score combining distributional alignment with a
   count-based coverage term can predict downstream task performance on
   diversity-reliant applications (e.g., data augmentation, creative writing,
   dialogue response generation).

MethodWe propose a three-component evaluation suite:

Component1: Semantic Distributional Divergence (SDD)

Given a reference corpus of human-written text and a generated corpus from a
model:

 1 Encode all utterances into a dense embedding space using a frozen sentence
   transformer (e.g., all-MiniLM-L6-v2).
 2 Project embeddings to a low-dimensional subspace via PCA (retaining95%
   variance) to mitigate the curse of dimensionality.
 3 Compute three distributional divergence measures on the projected embeddings:

 • Sliced Wasserstein Distance (SWD): Monte Carlo estimate over random
   projection directions. Interpretable as a "transport cost" between the two
   distributions.
 • Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD): Using a Gaussian RBF kernel with median
   heuristic bandwidth. Detects higher-order moment mismatches.
 • Fréchet Embedding Distance (FED): Analogous to FID in image generation — the
   Fréchet distance between two multivariate Gaussians fitted to the reference
   and generated embedding sets.

 4 Report the mean and standard deviation across10 bootstrap resamples of both
   corpora for uncertainty quantification.

   fraction of reference utterances for which the generated utterance is the
