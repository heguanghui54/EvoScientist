TitleA Multi-Task Framework for Automated Information Extraction from
Unstructured Scientific Text### ProblemExtracting structured relational
information (entities, attributes, relations) from unstructured scientific text
remains bottlenecked by two factors: (1) domain-specific entity types shift
across subfields, making rigid schemas brittle, and (2) annotated data is sparse
for most niche scientific domains. Current approaches either rely on large-scale
distant supervision (which introduces label noise) or require expensive expert
annotation per domain. A general-purpose framework that adapts to new scientific
domains with minimal supervision while maintaining extraction precision is
needed.

HypothesisA shared encoder-decoder architecture that jointly models named entity
recognition (NER), relation extraction (RE), and attribute-value extraction,
combined with a lightweight domain-adaptation module (adapter layers inserted
per domain), can transfer extraction capabilities across scientific subfields
and reach within5 F1 points of fully-supervised per-domain models using only10%
of the training data.

MethodThe framework—SciExtract—is a three-component pipeline:

 1 Encoder: A pretrained scientific language model (SciBERT) fine-tuned on a
   multi-task objective that combines NER, RE, and attribute extraction via a
   shared span representation.
 2 Multi-Task Decoder Heads: Three task-specific linear projection heads on top
   of the SciBERT [CLS] token and token-level span representations:

 • NER head: CRF-layer over token-level BIO labels.
 • RE head: Bilinear classifier over entity-pair span representations.
 • Attribute head: Question-answering formulation ("What is the [attribute] of
   [entity]?") with a span extraction head.

 3 Domain Adapters: Bottleneck adapter layers (down-project to d/4, up-project
   back to d) inserted after each transformer layer. When adapting to a new
   domain, only adapter parameters and task heads are fine-tuned; the base
   SciBERT is frozen.

Training proceeds in two stages:

 • Stage1 (multi-task pretraining): Train on three high-resource scientific
   domains (biomedical, computer science, materials science) jointly.
 • Stage2 (domain adaptation): For each new target domain, insert adapters and
   fine-tune on10% of available in-domain data.

Dataset / Benchmark- Training domains: BC5CDR (biomedical NER+RE), SciERC (CS
entities+relations), Materials-Schema (a synthetic materials science IE dataset
constructed from500 manually annotated papers sampled from the Materials Project
corpus).

 • Target evaluation domains: - Primary: Few-shot NER+RE on the ADE Corpus
   (adverse drug events, biomedical domain shift).
 • Secondary: Zero-shot transfer to CORD-19 subset (COVID-19 literature,200
   held-out documents with relation annotations from the CORD-NER+RE dataset).
