A Non-Evolving Single-Run Research Proposal## General-Purpose Audio Foundation
             Models via Multi-Objective Self-Supervised Learning---

TitleUniAudio-Base: A Scalable Audio Foundation Model Trained via Multi-Task
Self-Supervision Across Speech, Music, and Environmental Sound

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ProblemCurrent audio models are fragmented by domain. Speech systems (Whisper,
Hubert, WavLM) are pre-trained almost exclusively on spoken data and generalize
poorly to music or environmental audio. Music models (Jukebox, MERT) are trained
on music corpora and lack phoneme-level representations. Environmental-sound
models (BEATs, AudioMAE) capture broad acoustic events but fail on fine-grained
speech or musical pitch tasks. Building a single foundation model that works
across these three regimes remains unsolved for two structural reasons: (a)
heterogeneous data formats—sample rates differ (8 kHz–48 kHz), channel counts
vary (mono vs. stereo), and temporal resolutions required by downstream tasks
span three orders of magnitude (phonemes ~10 ms, musical notes ~100 ms, sound
events ~1 s); and (b) conflicting objectives—frame-level classification,
sequence-level contrastive learning, and generative reconstruction compete under
a shared trunk. Existing work avoids this by specializing; we treat it as the
central problem.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HypothesisA transformer trained with a multi-task self-supervised objective that
jointly optimizes (1) masked acoustic reconstruction (HuBERT-style), (2)
cross-modal contrastive alignment (text–audio, CLAP-style), and (3) a novel
temporal-resolution routing head that dynamically pools representations to match
task granularity, will match or exceed domain-specific baselines on speech
recognition, music instrument classification, and environmental sound event
detection simultaneously, without requiring per-domain fine-tuning of the trunk.

The key claim is that the routing head resolves the temporal-resolution
conflict, and that multi-task pre-training on a carefully balanced corpus of
speech, music, and environmental audio produces a shared representation space
that is richer than any single-domain objective can provide.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MethodArchitecture. A24-layer Conformer encoder (12 attention heads,768-dim,3072
FFN) with a learned relative positional encoding, operating on80-channel log-mel
spectrograms at three input resolutions (8 kHz,16 kHz,32 kHz). Inputs at
mismatched sample rates are resampled to the nearest supported rate with
antialiasing filters; the model learns a rate embedding that is added to each
frame.

Three pre-training heads, one shared trunk:

 1 **Masked Acoustic Reconstruction (MAR).**30% of time–frequency patches are
   masked; the model predicts the target cluster IDs derived from k-means on
   intermediate layer representations of a running teacher (HuBERT-style,
   iterative2-gram–4-gram clustering). Loss: cross-entropy over500 clusters.
   Applied to all three domains.
 2 Cross-Modal Contrastive Alignment (CMCA). For speech and music data with
   paired text (ASR transcripts, music caption tags), an auxiliary text encoder
   (distilroBERTa) projects descriptions into a shared256-dim space. A
   bidirectional InfoNCE loss pulls matching audio–text pairs together and
   pushes mismatched pairs apart. Temperature τ =0.07. No text for environmental
   sound (where captions are rare), so this head fires only on a subset of
   batches.
 3 Temporal-Resolution Routing Head (TRRH). A lightweight gating network
   (2-layer MLP,64-dim hidden) that takes the pooled per-frame output and
   predicts a pooling kernel width
