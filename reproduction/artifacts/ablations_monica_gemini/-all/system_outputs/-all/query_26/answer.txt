Title: Dynamic Curriculum Filtering for Domain-Specific Model
PretrainingProblem: General-purpose data filtering pipelines (deduplication,
heuristic quality scoring, perplexity filtering) fail for domain-specific
corpora because they cannot distinguish domain-relevant complexity from noise.
For example, biomedical text contains dense jargon and long-range dependencies
that general toxic-span or perplexity filters misclassify as low quality,
leading to high false-negative rates (40–60% reported in legal and clinical
domains). The core challenge is that domain corpora are small (typically1–10B
tokens), idiosyncratic, and lack the scale needed to train a reliable
general-domain filter. Existing solutions either require expensive human
annotation per domain or transfer poorly from general-domain classifiers.

Hypothesis: A two-stage curriculum filter that (1) learns domain-specific
quality signals via contrastive pairwise sampling from a small (≤5K) set of
domain expert judgments, then (2) sequences training data by a learned
anticipated learning gain proxy rather than by static quality scores, will
improve downstream task accuracy by ≥5% over both unfiltered and general-filter
baselines on three diverse domain-targeted benchmarks. The key mechanism is that
the curriculum stage adapts dynamically to the model's own learning trajectory,
reducing sensitivity to filter false negatives in early training.

domain experts (or synthetic judges prompted with domain rubrics): given two
documents, which is more useful for training? Train a small BERT-rank model
(110M params) to predict the preference logit. This yields a soft quality score
