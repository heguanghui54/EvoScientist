Mitigating Low-Bit Quantization Collapse via Activation-Aware Dynamic Precision
Scaling for Edge-Deployed LLMs## ProblemDeploying large language models on edge
devices (smartphones, IoT, embedded systems) requires aggressive quantization —
  typically4-bit or lower weights and activations — to fit within tight memory
 (4–8 GB) and power budgets. Standard post-training quantization (PTQ) methods
such as GPTQ, AWQ, and SmoothQuant apply uniform or static per-tensor bit-widths
      and rely on calibration sets that poorly approximate deployment-time
  distributions. Under4-bit weight-activation quantization, two failure modes
   dominate: (1) outlier-induced collapse — activations with large-magnitude
   channels saturate low-precision ranges, destroying signal in the remaining
   channels; and (2)cross-layer sensitivity mismatch — a single fragile layer
(e.g., early embeddings or late LM head) becomes a bottleneck when assigned the
same precision as robust layers. Existing work treats these separately, leading
  to asymmetric degradation: perplexity spikes of5–15 points on WikiText-2 and
      zero-shot accuracy drops of10–20% on reasoning benchmarks under W4A4
quantization. No current method jointly addresses per-layer outlier dynamics and
heterogeneous precision assignment in a single, hardware-aware, calibration-free
                                   framework.

HypothesisWe hypothesize that quantization collapse at extreme low-bit widths
can be mitigated by a two-stage framework that (1) dynamically rescales
activation outliers per-layer using a lightweight, input-dependent affine
transform learned from online statistics during a brief deployment adaptation
phase, and (2) assigns heterogeneous mixed-precision configurations via a greedy
sensitivity search over a convex hull of bit-widths (W2–W8, A4–A8), constrained
by an edge-device memory budget. Critically, we propose to jointly optimize the
rescaling parameters and the precision assignment in a single bilevel loop,
where the outer loop selects bit-widths and the inner loop optimizes per-channel
rescaling for the chosen configuration. This joint optimization is absent from
prior work and is expected to close the gap between uniform W4A4 and
full-precision performance to within2 perplexity points on held-out text.
